Step 4: the lighting runs 15 times per pixel

a frame
Pixels on screen (1440 × 900)1.30 M
Times the lighting shader ran19.1 M
Per pixel≈ 15

The terrain shader can discard a pixel (grass tufts, leaf cut-outs, dither fades), and a shader that can discard stops the GPU from testing depth before it runs. So every hillside, tree and wall behind what you finally see is fully lit, shadows and lamps and fog included, then overwritten. That is why each lighting part seemed to cost so much on its own (shadow lookups 4.6, night lights 3.1, lamps 2.6, bounce 2.2, cloud 1.7, fog 1.5 ms; textures and season tint about nothing): every part is paid about 15 times. The fix is a depth pre-pass: draw depth first with the cheap cut-out test, then light each pixel once.

The terrain pass at the bench, LOW (11.4 ms)

Each lighting part's cost is what turning it off alone saved. The parts are not additive: the shader is long, and taking any part out also lets the GPU run more pixels at once, so their sum is more than step 4. The distant night lights run by day because towns on the planet's night side count as lit, even past the horizon where no pixel can see them.

Read these numbers with care

  • The step 1–4 split is from the light view. The heavy views are next, at LOW and ULTRA.
  • On the light view the GPU is busy a third of the frame, so the card lowers its clocks: differences under about 0.1 ms are noise.
  • Builds are the fast profile, windowed, the player settings' defaults except where a level is named.
