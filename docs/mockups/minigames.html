Ways to make money on a station that are not hauling cargo between markets: a shift at the ore sorter, contracts off the board, and the games in the bar. Five of them are playable below with one wallet. Every game shows its odds and the house's edge on the table, because the player is owed the truth about a bet.

Earning, not betting

The job board

Contracts are posted by NPC companies and stations (and, later, other players). Pay sits in escrow when the contract is posted, and is released on delivery: a contract can never pay out money that nobody had.

Playable: a mining shift

pay set by Kessler's ore prices today

A wrong bin contaminates the hopper and is docked 3 cr; slag let through costs 2 cr. A good shift pays 80 to 140 cr: worse than hauling, better than nothing, and it needs no ship.

The truth about a bet

Odds and house edge

GameWhereReturn to playerHouse edgeTable limitNote

Every game here loses money on average, by design: gambling must never be a better job than a job. The variance is the point: a player who needs 400 cr for a repair tonight can risk 100 to get it. Limits are per station kind and per game day, so the casino cannot be farmed.

Where the money goes

Credits in, credits out

Proposed rule: the house's take is not destroyed outright. Most of it goes back into the world as wages (NPCs buying at the market, nudging prices) and as the station owner's budget, which posts bounties. A quarter leaves the economy as the cost of goods the station imports from off-system: that is the credit sink, and it scales with how much people gamble, not with a timer.

For the owner

Decisions / questions for the owner

1. Is there gambling in the game at all?

Options: (a) yes, with real odds shown; (b) only for in-game items, never credits; (c) none.

Recommendation: (a), with the odds printed on every table as here. It is a credit sink and a story generator. If the game will ever be rated for young players, gate it behind a setting and replace the casino with the job board's games.

2. Which minigames ship first?

The ore sorter, Reactor Roll, Hazard Dock, Orbit 21 and the race book are all playable here.

Recommendation: the ore sorter and Hazard Dock first (both tiny, both teach the economy), then the race book, because it can later use real NPC ships racing on real tracks round the station.

3. Can players play each other?

Options: house games only; player-versus-player card tables with a 5% rake; anything goes at the smuggler den.

Recommendation: house games first; player tables later at the den only, with the rake as the sink. Player-versus-player gambling needs the same durable transaction path as trades.

4. How big are the limits?

Recommendation: per-game limits by station kind (trade hub 200 cr, mining outpost 50 cr, smuggler den 2,000 cr), and a daily loss cap the player can set, which the game honours.

5. Do mining shifts pay from the market?

Recommendation: yes. The ore sorter's pay per rock is the outpost's buy price for that ore, scaled to a shift, so a rich vein event or a crash in iron shows up in the job's pay too.
