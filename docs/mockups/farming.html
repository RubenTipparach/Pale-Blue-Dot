Furrows and Greenhouses

Farming on hex cells: till a cell, plant a crop that suits the climate, keep it watered, wait out the days and harvest. Seasons decide what grows outdoors; a greenhouse lets anything grow anywhere, including on an airless moon. Pens hold the animals you tame. None of it is in the game yet.

Crops by climate

Each crop has a climate it grows in outdoors and the seasons it grows in. A year is 100 days (orbit-and-seasons), a day is 48 minutes, so a 6-day wheat crop takes about five hours of play. Inside a greenhouse every crop grows in every season. The four stage sprites are what the plot shows.

Soil, water and fertiliser

Tilling

The hoe turns a grass, sand or dirt cell into tilled soil. It is a top-block change, so it is a world edit on the durable path like digging (dig-and-place): the cell's top block becomes tilled, and an untended tilled cell goes back to dirt after 10 days.

Rock, ice and regolith cannot be tilled. On the moon you bring soil: a greenhouse is placed with tilled cells inside it.

Water and irrigation

A crop grows on a day it has water: watered by can that day, rained on, or irrigated, which is any cell within one cell of water. A channel dug from a river or pond keeps the cells beside it wet for good. That is a rule on the water cells around a crop, so it works today without the flow field water-flow leaves at zero; when water flows, a channel fed from upstream stays full.

Rice only grows on irrigated cells. Three dry days and a crop wilts: it stops growing, and a fourth kills it.

Fertiliser

Compost (from the pen's muck, crop scraps and the hearth's ash) makes a cell fertile: crops on it grow half again as fast and yield one more. It is used up at harvest.

Seasons, frost and greenhouses

Seasons

Day 0 is the northern summer; autumn is day 25, winter 50, spring 75. Outdoors, each crop grows only in its seasons. Winter frost kills every crop in the open except frost kale. The tropics (desert, jungle) barely have seasons, so their crops grow all year.

Rain falls on some days, more in spring and autumn, and waters every open cell. The weather would come from the real atmosphere (weather-rain); the mockup rolls a die.

Greenhouses

A greenhouse is a building of glass panes over a group of cells. Inside, the season and climate do not count: it is always growing weather. On a cold world that is how you eat in winter. On an airless body the greenhouse is sealed, holds air, and has grow lamps, since a pane alone does not make a farm on a moon. The glowcap needs no light at all and is the first crop a moon base grows.

Lit at night

Lanterns on posts round the plot, a grow lamp in the greenhouse, the farmhouse's window and door lamp, the pen's lamp. Each lights what is near it wherever the camera is; the moon stays dim. Crops do not need light to grow (except in a sealed greenhouse, where the grow lamp is the sun).

Pens and the harvest

Pens are fenced cells with a gate. Animals you tame (see the animals mockup: woolbacks, plodders, meadow hens, ridge goats, sand striders) are kept in them. Each morning a pen's hens lay, its plodders give milk, and its woolbacks grow fleece toward a shearing every 4 days. The plot above has a pen with three woolbacks and three hens; their produce lands in your harvest each day.

Harvest is the sickle on a ripe crop: the produce goes straight to the inventory with a count that pops, the cell stays tilled and dry, and any fertiliser is spent. A ripe crop left standing does not rot in this proposal; it waits for you. Produce stacks in the inventory grid like anything else (inventory-grid).

Saving: what the player did is stored: the tilled top blocks, each crop's species and the world time it was planted, its water days and fertiliser. Its stage is then a function of world time, so a farm costs nothing while you are away and simply has grown when you come back. World time passes only while the world is played.

About the art

The game's own textures: every hex top and side is the shipped terrain atlas (assets/tilesets/atlas.png), read the way the terrain shader reads it: each biome's sheet, its ground on top, the ground-into-earth tile on the first metre of a side, earth below. Tilled soil is the fields sheet's own tilled soil tile. Posts, fences, the trough, the farmhouse (plaster walls, a pitched thatch roof, boards) and the greenhouse frame (iron, fieldstone) are the settlement textures at their repeat_m. The penned woolbacks and hens are boxes in the animals mockup's pixel hides (animals/hide_*.png). The HUD is the game's: the monospace debug line and hotbar slots. All are byte-identical copies in docs/mockups/farming/game/, nearest-sampled.

Mockup-only: crop stages, produce, seed, tool and harvest icons are pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit, the solitaire collection's style (tools/art/farming_art.py): crop stages on a 32 px canvas, produce and tools on 24 px, Sweaft-64 colours only, alpha 0 or 255; the game has none of these yet. Watered and composted soil are the game's tilled tile with a tint, since the game has no wet or composted tile. The tilled tile exists only on the fields sheet, so the desert, jungle and tundra plots borrow it; each sheet would want its own. Glass and water are flat colour, as the game draws them. In the plot each crop is a pair of crossed sprites, the way the game's ground clutter is drawn: nearest-sampled without mipmaps and cut out by an alpha test, never blended.

Decisions / questions for the owner

  1. Do crops die? Recommendation: from frost and from four dry days only; never from neglect while you are away (time stops when the game is closed, so a crop's days are only played days).
  2. How fast? Recommendation: 3 to 7 game days to ripe (2.5 to 5.5 hours of play), so a crop is something you come back to, not something you watch.
  3. Is irrigation just "next to water"? Recommendation: yes for now; when water flows (voxel-engine-foundation), require the channel to be fed, so a dry ditch does nothing.
  4. Greenhouse as a building or as cells? Recommendation: a placed building from the building kit (player-building), whose footprint marks its cells as sheltered; sealed if it has a door with an airlock.
  5. Do towns farm on their own? Recommendation: yes, town fields (already drawn in tenebris-towns) grow and are harvested by townsfolk, as changes the world makes that never overwrite the player's.
  6. Crops per planet? Recommendation: like animals, an explicit roster per body; a crop from one world grows elsewhere only as seed you carried, and only in a greenhouse.
