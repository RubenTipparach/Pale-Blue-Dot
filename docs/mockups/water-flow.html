Water that flows

All Pale Blue Dot mockups (the tracker)

Today the game's water is one still material, filled up to sea level when the world is made, and nothing ever moves it. This page runs Luanti's liquid rule, ported to our hex cells, on a patch with a sea, a beach, a plain with a dry basin, a ledge and a plateau. Pour a source, dig a channel, drop a block in the stream. Water falls first, then spreads seven cells, a level lower each cell, and recedes when its source goes. It moves one tick a second, as Luanti's does, and only after Run water or a preset.

The rule

Luanti's liquid transform, on eight neighbours

Each cell of water is a source or a flowing cell with a level from 7 (beside a source) to 0 (the thinnest film), and a flag for falling. Only queued cells are looked at. An edit queues a cell and its neighbours, and a cell that changes queues the neighbours it can reach. A tick takes the queue once.

Hex

Eight neighbours, not six

Up, six sides and down, in Luanti's order (upper, then the sides, then lower). A pentagon cell has five sides and the same rule.

Hex

Renew at three

Two hex cells side by side share two neighbours, so Luanti's threshold of two floods any plain from a single pair (the preset shows it). Three fills a pool's corners and leaves a row or a triangle of sources alone, which is what two of four does on Luanti's squares.

Hex

Seven cells is 20 m

A cell is 2.833 m across, so a sheet spreads 20 m from its source where Luanti's spreads 7 m. The range slider shortens it. Each flowing cell's top stands at (level + 1) / 8 of its 1 m layer, a flat hexagon, so a sheet steps down by eighths.

Saves

Only edits start water

Generated water is all source and never queued, so opening a world never moves its water. Sources are saved as edits; settled flowing water follows from the sources and the ground, which "Settle from scratch" checks.
