About this mockup: the owner's words, the idea, and which art is the game's
"Mock up food recipes, bakery (more farming?) stir fry, pizza, noodles, pasta, different kinds of meat, and fish, calamari and shrimp (more sea life?)" (the owner, 2026-11-14)

Cooking on Tenebris (the home world, in the Aubade system) as a system: the kitchen stations from a campfire to a ship's galley, built and placed on the hex grid like any station in the crafting mockup (M3); recipes with real chains, from the field (M8's crops and twelve new ones), the pen (M7's animals; domestication and breeding are the husbandry mockup's, drafted alongside this one) and the sea (the fish the game catches today and M25's water life) through the quern, the churn, the barrel and the smoker to the dish; and what food does once it is eaten. Four kitchens are 3D scenes on the game's 2.833 m cells and 1 m layers, by day and lit at night only by their own fires, ovens, lanterns and candles. Drag to look, scroll to zoom, shift-drag to pan; click a station to cook at it. Fire flickers and smoke rises because the world moves them; nothing else moves on its own.

Today in the game: nothing here is built. The only food-like thing the game has is a caught fish, an Item::Fish(u16) that stacks to 16 and does nothing; there is no hunger, health, breath or cold, and the HUD deliberately shows no status bar for a mechanic that does not exist (hotbar-and-game-hud). What food would change is chosen from what the game does have: sprinting and swimming, digging, fishing and lamps.

About the art. The game's own (byte-identical copies in cooking/game/, see its README): every ground cell outside is a tile of the terrain atlas (assets/tilesets/atlas.png, the fields sheet), drawn as planet_surface.wgsl picks a column's top and side; walls, floors, ovens, hearths, tables, shelves, barrels and stoves wear the settlement textures (brick, clay, stone, flagstone, cobble, plank, boards, timber, plaster, whitewash, tile, slate, iron, barrel, linen) at their repeat_m; the fish are the game's item icons (assets/items/fish), and so are the candle, lanterns and hand. From other mockups (byte-identical, each folder has its source): M3's materials (cooking/m3/), M7's animals and their meat, milk and egg drops (cooking/m7/), M8's seven crops (cooking/m8/) and M25's squid, shrimp, octopus, clams, lobster, crabs and kelp (cooking/m25/). Mockup-only: every dish, ingredient, meat cut, fillet, kitchen station icon, the twelve new crops and their growth stages, the three proposed catchables and the kitchen textures and cards (fire, embers, crust, dough, hanging herbs, fish and noodles) are pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit (tools/art/cooking_art.py, Sweaft-64, hard alpha), sampled nearest with no mipmaps. Stations and props are boxes and hex prisms, the way the game builds things; food on a table is a pixel card.

How it was checked. The screenshots in docs/screenshots/mockup-cooking/ were rendered in a cloud container with no GPU (headless Chromium on SwiftShader), so they show the look, never how smoothly it runs.

The recipe book

Every recipe in this proposal: what it makes, where, how long and how hot, what goes in, what it does once eaten and how long it keeps. Times are seconds at the station (a job runs only while the world is played, as M3's do). Keeps is in game days: a day is 48 minutes of play (orbit-and-seasons), and "keeps" is how long until stale, so a 1-day fish wants cooking or salting within the hour you caught it. Effects are explained under What food does.

Field to plate

Where every dish comes from: what grows, grazes and swims on the left, what the quern, the churn, the barrel, the press and the rack make of it in the middle, and the dish on the right. Hover or tab to a node to light up what it needs and what it feeds; the line's colour is the station that makes the step. Pick a family to see its chains alone.

New crops (the "more farming?")

Twelve crops the recipes need, drawn in M8's layout (four stages on a 32 px canvas: the seeded mound, sprout, growing, ripe; the produce on 24 px) so they sit in M8's plot beside wheat, rice, chili, turnip, sun melon, frost kale and glowcap. They follow M8's rules unchanged: a climate and seasons outdoors, water each day, frost kills all but the hardy, a greenhouse grows anything anywhere. This is a proposal to M8, not an edit to it.

How they join M8

Appended to M8's crop roster, which is append-only like the fish roster (a planted crop is saved by its index). Each is a seed the player plants; none appears in the ground on its own, so no generator changes and no save is touched: seeds come from the farmer and the trader (M5) and from the market (M12).

Two are perennials, a rule M8 does not have yet: olive trees and tea bushes stay after harvest and fruit again (olives every 6 days, tea every 3). A young olive is killed by frost; a grown one is not.

Two sugars, by climate

Sugar cane grows hot and wet; sugar beet grows temperate and cold. Both boil down at the hearth to the same sugar, so a cold-world baker is never stuck. The same goes for oil: olives on hot dry coasts, soy in the temperate belt; both go through the press.

On Sequoia and the moons

Crops are an explicit roster per planet (M8's recommendation; CLAUDE.md: no accidental species fallback). These are Tenebris's. On Sequoia, Tenebris seed grows only in a greenhouse; Sequoia's own food (its fungal forest's rose caps, its tan-sod grains) waits for its own roster. On an airless body, only the sealed greenhouse and the galley's stores feed you.

Meat, milk and eggs

Which animal gives what, and what each becomes. The animals are M7's; taming, pens, breeding and new domestic stock are the husbandry mockup's, drafted alongside this one, so this page only names the products. M7's one meat drop becomes a cut by animal, and a cut has four states: raw (keeps a day), cooked (two days), smoked and cured (a month). A gap where M7 has no animal for a meat the owner named is marked.

From the husbandry draft (animal-husbandry, drafted alongside; its names may change): it proposes a domestic pig, the rootling (from the tusk boar), which would be the pork here; yard hens and yard dabblers for hen and duck eggs; and clover bees, whose honey would sweeten as sugar does (a honey recipe waits for that draft to land). Meat comes from its butcher's block as these cuts; every dish made from them is this page's.

Hunting stays a choice (M7): everything but meat and hide comes from a tamed animal without killing it. A player who never hunts eats eggs, milk, butter, cheese, bread, pasta, fish and everything from the field, and misses only the meat dishes.

Fish and seafood

The fish the game catches today are the first real ingredient: any caught fish cuts into two fillets by hand. M25's water life gives the rest. Squid is calamari; the shrimp, prawns, clams, mussels, lobster, crab and crayfish go to the pot, the grill and the fryer. Three catchables are missing for the dishes the owner named, and are proposed below (to M25, not edited into it).

Raw fish

Sushi takes only a fish that is safe raw: silverfin, deepback and redrun (cold, clean water). M25 already says the bloatfin is poisonous unless cleaned; it is never served raw, and cleaning it is a recipe of its own for later. The kelp frond, cut from M25's forests and dried on the rack, is the sheet that wraps it.

What food does

Meals give buffs; nothing drains

There is no hunger bar. Eating a dish gives its effect for some minutes of play; up to three dishes work at once (a full belly), and a fourth replaces the one with least time left. The same effect does not stack: the best one counts. Not eating costs nothing.

Why: the game is a calm explorer with no death, no health and no survival clock; world time passes only while it is played, and a long flight to Sequoia should not be nagged by a stomach. A hunger bar punishes the player for doing something else; a buff rewards the player who cooks. The HUD gains buff icons only once there are buffs (hotbar-and-game-hud: no bar for a mechanic that does not exist).

Freshness and keeping

Every food stack remembers the world time it was made; its age is played time, never time with the game closed. Past its keep it is stale (it still cooks, at a lower star), and at twice its keep it spoils into compost (M8's fertiliser), so nothing just vanishes. When two stacks of the same food merge, the stack keeps the older time.

Keeping it longer: salt (the barrel, a month), smoke (the smoker, a month), drying (the rack: jerky, pasta, tea, kelp, forever), pickling and brine (two months), ice (an icebox fourfold; on a frozen world, a box outdoors in winter does the same), and cans (the galley, forever, for long voyages).

Quality, without dice

One to three stars, worked out, never rolled (M3: the same recipe always makes the same thing, so a multiplayer server has one rule to check):

  • the freshest band of the oldest ingredient: fresh +2, good +1, stale +0;
  • the right heat for the recipe: +1;
  • practice: +1 once you have cooked it five times.

Four points is three stars, two or three is two, less is one. Stars stretch the effect (x1, x1.5, x2) and the price.

Learning recipes

A new world knows the campfire's basics. The rest is learned: from townsfolk (the innkeeper's stew and pies, the fisher's chowder and calamari, the farmer's bread and cheese, a baker and a noodle cook proposed as new town roles to M5), from recipe books sold at stalls and found in ruins, and by discovery: put a recipe's ingredients in a station you know and it is learned. Known recipes are part of the save, a list of ids.

Selling

Food sells to the innkeeper and at market stalls, priced by its stars and freshness. Towns pay for what their land does not grow (a desert town for fish, a coast for bread). Stations and outposts grow nothing, so provisions (cans, hardtack, jerky, cheese) are proposed as a ninth good on M12's exchange: a hauler's run from a farming world to a station.

Saves and the inventory

A food is a new Item::Food(u16), an index into an append-only food roster, as Item::Fish(u16) is; a stack adds its made-at world time and its stars. Today's caught fish stay Fish and are an ingredient as they are, so no save migrates: a world with no food in it opens exactly as before. A placed kitchen station is a stored record on the durable path like M3's, and a cooking job advances only while the world is played.
