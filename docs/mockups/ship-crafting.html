Shipyard and Ship Builder

All Pale Blue Dot mockups (the tracker)

Ships made from modules on a grid, in a shipyard you walk around. Every number a ship flies with follows from its parts: mass from what you bolted on, acceleration from its thrusters, how fast it turns from its gyros and how its mass is spread. Those numbers are the game's own FlightLimits, so a built ship flies by the same assisted Newtonian rules the survey ship does today.

About the art. The game's own (byte-identical copies in ship-crafting/game/, see its README): the hex ground is the terrain atlas's fields sheet (assets/tilesets/atlas.png) on 2.833 m hex columns with 1 m layers; the apron, pads, pad rims, boardwalk, hangar walls and roof are the settlement textures granite, iron, stone, plank and slate at their repeat_m; the kestrel, the tern and the loon parked by the bay are the game's vehicle models (assets/models/vehicles); the hand in the hotbar is the game's icon. Mockup-only: the module icons and the ships' hull textures are pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit, the solitaire collection's style (tools/art/ship-crafting_art.py): 32 x 32 sprites shown at whole-number scales and 32 x 32 tiles sampled nearest with no mipmaps. The ships are boxes built from the builder's own grid, one 2 m block a module, so what you draw below is exactly what rolls out on the pad.

1The shipyard

Four example ships on their pads and a build bay between two gantries, where your design from section 2 rolls out. Drag to look around, scroll or pinch to zoom. Set the time to night: every pad light, floodlight, hangar window, nav light and thruster lights what is near it, wherever the camera is, and the moon stays dim enough that they read.

3How the numbers follow from the parts

  
The flight code already takes a FlightLimits: a mode speed, an acceleration, an angular speed and acceleration, and linear and angular damping (the inertial and rotational dampeners). Today they are code defaults. A built ship computes its own, once, when it is launched, from the formulas below; the flight code does not change.

  

    
FlightLimits fieldFrom the partsWhy

    
      
accelerationmain thrust / massNewton. More cargo pods means a slower ship unless you add thrusters.

      
angular_accelerationgyro torque / yaw inertia, at most 6 rad/s²Inertia is each module's mass times its distance from the centre of mass squared, so a long freighter turns slower than a compact shuttle with the same gyros.

      
angular_speed0.3 + 0.6 √α, at most the default 1.5 rad/sA comfort cap for the pilot; the physics limit is the torque. Mouse look stays raw: this limits the ship, never the camera.

      
linear_dampingRCS thrust / mass / 5, at most 1.0The inertial dampener can only null drift with thrust it has. It is off when the pilot turns it off (X), as now.

      
angular_damping1.4 α, at most the default 4.0The rotational dampener uses the same gyros that turn the ship.

      
speednot a part stat: the flight mode's targetMomentum is Newtonian; the mode speed is what the assist aims for, not a top speed.

      
lift-offlift jets ≥ 1.1 × mass × 25 m/s²25 m/s² is the game's 1 g (SURFACE_GRAVITY_MPS2_PER_G). A ship that cannot lift off at 1 g still flies; it lives in orbit and docks.

    
  

  
No patched conics, maneuver nodes or n-body ship simulation come with this (CLAUDE.md). Ships keep local gravity falloff and the dampeners; planets stay on rails.

6Saves, and what stays true

  

    
A ship is a stored record
Its stable ID, its grid, its pose in a named frame, its hold and its damage are saved the moment the ship is launched and every time they change. Leaving the seat changes who is in it, never whether it exists.

    
The grid is versioned
A saved ship names its module set's version. A module whose stats are retuned keeps the old numbers for ships already built, or the change migrates them forward on open; a removed module stays readable. A player's ship never vanishes or changes size.

    
Rebuilding refunds
Taking a module off at a fabricator gives its parts back to the pack by the give order, with drops for what does not fit, so redesigning costs time, not materials.
