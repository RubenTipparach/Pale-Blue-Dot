Pale Blue Dot, a design for trading

The exchange terminal

Every station keeps its own stock of eight goods, and its prices follow that stock: what it makes piles up and gets cheap, what it eats runs short and gets dear. NPC haulers move goods toward the price gaps, and events (a mine collapse, a blight, a navy exercise) push them apart again. Five companies are listed; their shares follow the goods they live on. The market below is running: buy here, dock somewhere else, sell.

Proposal, not built  Nothing here is in the engine. Travel is instant in this mockup, so arbitrage is easier than it will be. Icons and company marks are pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit, the solitaire collection's style (tools/art/market_art.py): 28 x 28 sprites shown at 1x and 2x, nearest neighbour.

How it works

The model behind the numbers

Price from stock

Each station has a target stock for each good. Mid price = base × (target / stock)0.6, held between 0.3x and 4x base. Buy and sell sit either side of the mid by the station's spread: 6% at a trade hub, 14% at the smuggler den.

Production, consumption, haulers

Every game hour a station makes and eats goods at its own rates. NPC haulers move a little stock from where a good is cheap to where it is dear, which ties prices together without making them equal. The player's trades move stock exactly as an NPC's do.

Events

An event changes a station's rates for a few days. The wire announces it when it happens, so a player who reads the news can get there first. Events are drawn by the world, never by the player's position, and the draw is seeded so it replays.

Shares

Each company has a fair value computed from the goods it sells and buys (Kessler from ore and ice, Ventra from fuel less ore). The price drifts toward fair value with noise, and events hit fair value directly. Players can only buy what is listed; no shorting in this design.

Persistence

Stocks, prices, contracts and share holdings are stored state on the durable path, with authors: a trade by the player, a delivery by an NPC hauler. The world only advances while it is played. A player's own sell listing is a stored record that outlives the session.

One rule, two players

The market is one authoritative simulation on the host. A trade is a transaction: it commits or it does not. Two players buying the last 10 t at once cannot both get it.

For the owner

Decisions / questions for the owner

1. How much do players know about other stations' prices?

Options: (a) everything, live; (b) what they saw last time they docked, plus news; (c) a paid data service.

Recommendation: (b), with (c) as an upgrade sold by the research station. Stale prices are what make trading a game rather than a spreadsheet.

2. Can players move prices?

Recommendation: yes, by the same stock rule as NPCs. Dumping 60 t of iron on Kessler Rock should crash it; that keeps one good route from being farmed forever.

3. How far does the stock market go?

Options: buy and sell listed shares only; plus dividends; plus shorting and options; plus player-founded companies.

Recommendation: buy and sell plus a quarterly dividend. No shorting. Player companies later, tied to owning an outpost.

4. Player-to-player trade

Options: direct trades when docked together; a station order book; contracts with escrow.

Recommendation: sell listings on a station's order book, and contracts with escrow, both durable records. Direct trades later, once multiplayer exists.

5. Are there illegal goods?

Recommendation: yes, per jurisdiction: spice is legal at the Meridian and contraband at Gatehouse Kappa. The den buys anything. It gives the customs scan on the stations page a reason to exist.
