About this mockup: the owner's words, the idea, and which art is the game's
"Draft up mockups for advanced ocean environments" ... "Coral reefs, clams, lobsters, crabs, bottom feeders. More marine life. Octopus, squid, tube worm thingies on vents" ... "Oh yea, rivers are important too, like beavers and dams, kelp forests, marshes" ... "And ducks" (the owner, 2026-11-14)

Every water place Tenebris makes (the home planet, in the Aubade system), as the game would have it: what makes each one distinct to look at, swim and sail through, what lives there and moves, what the player does there, and what in the planet's data decides where it is. Ten places, each a 3D scene on the world's own cell (2.833 m across, 1 m layers: seabeds terrace, reefs, ice, dams and lodges are cells), by day and at night, lit at night only by their own lights: bioluminescence, vent glow, a diver's lamp, lanterns, a lighthouse, fireflies; the moon stays dim. Then how light behaves under water, a bestiary of the water life, a chart of where each place occurs on a sample planet, and how it maps onto the engine. Drag to look, scroll to zoom, W A S D to swim the view and R/F to rise and sink. Creatures move because they are alive; nothing else in a view moves on its own.

About the art. The game's own (byte-identical copies in ocean/game/, see its README): every ground, seabed, ice and mud cell is a tile of the terrain atlas (assets/tilesets/atlas.png: the ocean, beach, frozen, swamp, basalt, fields and mountains sheets), drawn as planet_surface.wgsl picks a column's top, side, earth and stone; the lighthouse, boat, wreck, hut, chimneys and ice blocks wear the settlement textures (stone, timber, plank, iron, driftwood, linen, slate, basalt, ice, snow, peat, leaves, bark) at their repeat_m; the eight fish that swim in the game today use their own item icons (assets/items/fish). From other mockups: the heron, gull, shore crab, bog toad, mire lurker, firefly and glowmoth portraits and the feather, egg, shell, meat, glow and hide drop icons are M7's (animals/); bloatfin, lanternjaw, glassback, emberback and sunplate are M9's (fish/). Mockup-only: every other creature, the coral, kelp, reed, lily, fan and mangrove cards and ten textures (brain and table coral, sponge, bacterial mat, mussel bed, tube worm tube and plume, beaver sticks, wake) are pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit (tools/art/ocean_art.py, Sweaft-64), sampled nearest with no mipmaps. Creatures are pixel cards (side views turned along their heading, crawlers laid flat), the cheap way the game would draw hundreds of them. The water, light, caustics, god rays and marine snow are shaders written for this page; the "in the engine" tab says which of them the game has today.

How it was checked. The screenshots in docs/screenshots/mockup-ocean/ were rendered in a cloud container with no GPU (Chromium on SwiftShader), so they show the look, never how smoothly it runs.

How underwater looks

What this shows, and how to use it

A diver goes down a reef wall that drops from the surface to the floor of the world's column, 145 m down (column::BASE_M, which never moves). A colour board hangs two metres in front of the lens: red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue. Drag the depth: red goes first (it is gone by ten metres), then orange and yellow, and by the twilight zone only blue is left. Switch between today's game (the sea's one absorption, absorption_per_m 0.32 / 0.035 / 0.012, and a murk lit by the open sea's light whatever the depth) and proposed (light that fades with depth, caustics on the bed, god rays near the surface, marine snow, and a turbidity per place). The chart beside it is the light left at each depth.

Saves, and what stays true

New places are a new generator version

Reefs, kelp beds, vents, deeper trenches, stepped rivers, marsh pools and sea ice cells change what a world is made of, so they ship as a new terrain generator version, for new worlds. A world keeps the version it names, cell for cell; the old version is carried forever with its digest test (openspec/specs/planet/terrain, "The generator is versioned"). Opening an old world never rebuilds it onto the new seabed.

Habitat is derived, a fished-down stock is a record

Where a reef or a kelp bed is, and which species can spawn there now, is derived from the seed, the generator version and the live climate, and never saved (world-persistence decision 1). A named fishing ground, a pearl bed someone has worked, or a stock that can be fished down is a stored record, and changes through the journal.

A beaver dam is an authored world change

A colony's dam, lodge and felled trees are journal entries authored @wbeaver:<colony>, written through the durable path like the player's digging, and checked first against every cell the player has touched: the world never overwrites the player. Break a dam and the pond drains; the colony does not rebuild on the cells you broke, it moves its dam to the next site.

Every depth fits the column

A saved edit is indexed from column::BASE_M (−145 m), which does not move. So the deep is fitted to the column: sunlit to 40 m, twilight to 90 m, midnight to the floor, set by how fast light fades with depth, not by real-ocean metres. No old save gains a layer it cannot index.
