Foliage detail playground

A proposal, not built behaviour. The owner asked for "a new foliage detail mockup too so I can play around with detailed envs". This page is that: a patch of hex columns to tune ground cover on, by biome, season and time of day. None of it is in the game. What the game draws today is the Tenebris hex-prism tree (the "Tenebris hex prisms" choice under Trees) and the clutter in tenebris-ground-clutter.

The art is painted by code

Every texture, leaf card, impostor and species icon here was painted by a script, tools/art/foliage_art.py, with the hand-drawn-illustration kit, copying your friend's method: a flat block, hatching in the shape's own tones, form crescents, highlight dabs and a same-hue edge. It is not hand drawn and not your friend's work. The page samples it nearest, as the game samples terrain.

How it stays consistent with the plans

  • No pop-in (priority 1). Every layer switches level with a screen-door Bayer 4 x 4 dither that crosses over a ring of width "fade width" times the ring's radius, ending at the line, as detail-fade and distance-lod-fade design it: the outgoing level keeps the pixels the incoming one gives up, so there is never a frame where both or neither show. The last level fades to nothing before the draw distance. Set Switching to "Hard cut" to see the pop it replaces, and use Fly-by to watch it in motion. The default fade width is 0.3, the shipped lod_fade_width.
  • Trees stand one per cell, at the cell centre, as Tenebris places them, at the biome rates of tenebris-tree-geometry scaled by the slider. The hex-prism style is that change's geometry: a 0.20 trunk, two leaf layers at 0.65 + 0.35 x hash of the cell, a pine as one wood block under a cone of layers 0.95 down to 0.15, never rotated off the grid.
  • Grass blades take their colour from a narrow slice of the ground tile they grow on, as Tenebris's scatter does; the mid ring swaps to a painted card, and past that the ground tile carries the colour.
  • Biomes are big (bigger-biomes): one patch is one biome, about a tenth of a 750 m biome's width.
  • Lit at night. Lanterns along the path, a campfire, glowing fungi, crystals and fireflies each light what is near them wherever the camera is. The static lights are baked into each terrain vertex and each plant, as the towns mockup bakes block light; the 16 fireflies move, so they are summed in the shader, all of them, every frame. The moon is kept dim so the lamps read.

What it leaves out

  • Distances are scaled to the patch. The game's rings are hundreds of metres (trees fade out at about 1.2 to 2.4 km); here they are tens, so the whole ladder fits on screen.
  • No shadow maps. Trees darken the ground and plants under their crowns with a baked occlusion term instead.
  • Changing a density or the biome rebuilds the patch, which can add plants in one frame. In the game the scatter is a pure function of the cell, so that never happens in play.
  • Frame time here is a browser's, not the engine's. The counters are for comparing settings against each other, not for budgets.
