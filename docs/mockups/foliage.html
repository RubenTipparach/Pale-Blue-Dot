Foliage detail playground

A proposal, not built behaviour. The owner asked for "a new foliage detail mockup too so I can play around with detailed envs". This page is that: a patch of hex columns to tune ground cover on, by biome, season and time of day. None of it is in the game. What the game draws today is the Tenebris hex-prism tree (the "Tenebris hex prisms" choice under Trees) and the clutter in tenebris-ground-clutter.

Whose textures these are

The game's own, wherever it has one (byte-identical copies in docs/mockups/foliage/game/, sources named in its README):

  • Terrain: assets/tilesets/atlas.png, sampled as planet_surface.wgsl's pixel_tile does (32 texels a tile, nearest). Each biome draws its game sheet: the top is tile (0,0); a wall is the sod-into-earth side (1,0) for its first metre, the earth (2,0) down to 4 m, then the stone (3,0); sand biomes show their own sand to 4 m; snow is the tundra sheet's (2,2). Top UVs are the game's: the cell's local position over 1.5 tile widths.
  • The game's trees (Tree geometry: Tenebris hex prisms): wood is the biome sheet's tile (2,1) and leaves its (0,2), as material codes 8 and 9 are. The detailed trees use the same bark and leaf tiles, plus needles, leaves and sage from assets/textures/settlement/.
  • Grass blades crop a vertical strip 0.18 wide of their ground tile, as the clutter pass does. Rocks and pebbles are the sheet's stone, logs its bark, reeds and litter the settlement reed and litter, the lanterns timber and iron. The fungal forest is the game's sporewood sheet (stalk, cap skin, spore clusters), the crystals the frozen wastes sheet's ice and frost crystals, the kelp the ocean sheet's kelp mat.

Mockup-only: pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit, the solitaire collection's style (tools/art/foliage_art.py: native 16 to 56 px canvases, Sweaft-64 colours, short ramps lit from the top left, a 1 px outline, alpha 0 or 255, sampled nearest and alpha-tested), because the game has no texture for them: flower heads and the glowing bloom, red and brown mushroom caps, birch and palm bark, and the species icons. The painted tree impostors are only a placeholder for the moment before the bake below finishes. A card's leaf, needle, frond or blade shape is cut from the game tile by a pixel mask in the shader, so no leaf texture is drawn at all.

Mid and far trees are baked from the near tree. Once a biome's textures have loaded, every tree variant's real near model (its branches, needle sprays and game textures, in the current season, bare in winter) is rendered orthographically from 8 bearings into a strip of 48 x 72 px views, read back, snapped to the Sweaft-64 palette with alpha 0 or 255, and given to that variant's mid cards (four crossed planes, each showing the view baked from its own side) and far billboard (the view nearest the camera's bearing). Near, mid and far are one tree, so the cross-fade between them never changes what the tree is.

Leaf cards hang from their branches. Every leaf, needle, frond and willow card has its root edge on the branch it grows from and points out along it. A wind mask, zero at the root and one at the tip (the square of the distance along the card), scales the card's own flutter, so the root stays pinned while the tree's sway moves branch and card together. "Show wind mask" colours cards black at the root and white at the tip.

How it stays consistent with the plans

  • No pop-in (priority 1). Every layer switches level with a screen-door Bayer 4 x 4 dither in which the outgoing level keeps exactly the pixels the incoming one gives up, so there is never a frame where both or neither show. By default the switch is timed: when a plant crosses a ring (with 4% hysteresis) its two levels cross-fade over the fade time, then the new level is solid, so a dither is only ever on screen while a fade is in progress. Changing a setting fades the new plants in the same way. "Distance cross-fade band" is the distance-lod-fade design (a standing dither in a band of width "fade width" times the ring, the shipped lod_fade_width 0.3), kept to compare; "Hard cut" shows the pop both replace. Fly-by shows them in motion.
  • Trees stand one per cell, at the cell centre, as Tenebris places them, at the biome rates of tenebris-tree-geometry scaled by the slider. The hex-prism style is that change's geometry: a 0.20 trunk, two leaf layers at 0.65 + 0.35 x hash of the cell, a pine as one wood block under a cone of layers 0.95 down to 0.15, never rotated off the grid.
  • Grass blades take their colour from a narrow slice of the ground tile they grow on, as Tenebris's scatter does; the mid ring swaps to a crossed card cut from the same tile, and past that the ground tile carries the colour.
  • Biomes are big (bigger-biomes): one patch is one biome, about a tenth of a 750 m biome's width.
  • Lit at night. Lanterns along the path, a campfire, glowing fungi, crystals and fireflies each light what is near them wherever the camera is. The static lights are baked into each terrain vertex and each plant, as the towns mockup bakes block light; the 16 fireflies move, so they are summed in the shader, all of them, every frame. The moon is kept dim so the lamps read.

What it leaves out

  • Distances are scaled to the patch. The game's rings are hundreds of metres (trees fade out at about 1.2 to 2.4 km); here they are tens, so the whole ladder fits on screen.
  • No shadow maps. Trees darken the ground and plants under their crowns with a baked occlusion term instead.
  • Changing a density or the biome rebuilds the patch; the new plants fade in rather than appear. In the game the scatter is a pure function of the cell, so it does not change in play.
  • Frame time here is a browser's, not the engine's. The counters are for comparing settings against each other, not for budgets.
