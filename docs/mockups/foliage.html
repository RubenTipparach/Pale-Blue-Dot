Foliage detail playground

A playground for detailed ground cover on a hex patch: 2.833 m cells, 1 m steps. Pick a biome, tune the layers, scrub the clock into night. Nothing here is built in the game yet.

A proposal, not built behaviour. The owner asked for "a new foliage detail mockup too so I can play around with detailed envs". This page is that: a patch of hex columns to tune ground cover on, by biome, season and time of day. None of it is in the game. What the game draws today is the Tenebris hex-prism tree (the "Tenebris hex prisms" choice under Trees) and the clutter in tenebris-ground-clutter.

Whose textures these are

The game's own, wherever it has one (byte-identical copies in docs/mockups/foliage/game/, sources named in its README):

  • Terrain: assets/tilesets/atlas.png, sampled as planet_surface.wgsl's pixel_tile does (32 texels a tile, nearest). Each biome draws its game sheet: the top is tile (0,0); a wall is the sod-into-earth side (1,0) for its first metre, the earth (2,0) down to 4 m, then the stone (3,0); sand biomes show their own sand to 4 m; snow is the tundra sheet's (2,2). Top UVs are the game's: the cell's local position over 1.5 tile widths.
  • Grass: pixel tufts, the default (owner, 2026-11-14: "this grass is actually better than what we have in the game"; 2026-11-15: "first default to pixel tufts, make sure its color matches the ground though"). The Play demo's tuft (Stone and Timber, ui/play.js): nine one-pixel blades on a 16 px card, dark root to light tip, on two crossed cards 0.8 m across and 0.6 m tall, sampled nearest. Each biome's three tones are read off its own ground tile, the one its terrain top wears: the tile's 1,024 texels sorted by brightness and split in thirds, each third giving its commonest colour, so the tufts are the ground's own dark, mid and light. They sway in the wind and turn to straw under snow. The Grass switch still offers the blades cut from the ground tile. Nothing here is in the game.
  • The game's trees (Tree geometry: Tenebris hex prisms): wood is the biome sheet's tile (2,1) and leaves its (0,2), as material codes 8 and 9 are. The detailed trees use the same bark and leaf tiles, plus needles, leaves and sage from assets/textures/settlement/.
  • Grass blades crop a vertical strip 0.18 wide of their ground tile, as the clutter pass does. Rocks and pebbles are the sheet's stone, logs its bark, reeds and litter the settlement reed and litter, the lanterns timber and iron. The fungal forest is the game's sporewood sheet (stalk, cap skin, spore clusters), the crystals the frozen wastes sheet's ice and frost crystals, the kelp the ocean sheet's kelp mat.

One art style, no outlines in the world (owner, 2026-11-15: "outlines are for sprites in the ui inventory system, there is no need for that in the world, this game looks much better without harsh outlines"). Every plant and prop is drawn in its texture's palette but pulled toward that texture's own mean colour (leaf, needle, frond, litter and bush cards keep 45% of their contrast, bark, rock, logs, caps and stalks 70%; the ground keeps all of it), so a tree reads as a soft mass lit like the ground rather than salt-and-pepper speckle, and the baked impostors' light and dark bands are gentle. The biome picker's icons are UI and keep their outline.

Mockup-only: pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit (tools/art/foliage_art.py: native 16 to 56 px canvases, Sweaft-64 colours, short ramps lit from the top left, alpha 0 or 255, sampled nearest and alpha-tested), because the game has no texture for them: flower heads and the glowing bloom, red and brown mushroom caps, birch and palm bark, and the species icons. The world's versions (flowers, impostors, birch bark) are redrawn without the outline ring or seams by foliage/world_art.py.

Crops and gardens (owner, 2026-11-15: "make sure to add plants and farmable stuff in foliage thing too"): M8's crops, in the same outline-free world stages the farming mockup's plot draws (farming/world_art.py writes both copies), on small plots of the fields sheet's tilled soil at all four stages (seeded, sprout, growing, ripe), and a few gone wild. Which crop grows where is M8's: temperate wheat, turnip and frost kale; jungle chili, rice and sun melon; savanna and desert sun melon and chili; taiga frost kale and turnip; swamp rice and turnip; glowcaps in the fungal forest, under its roof of caps; none on the crystal or kelp worlds.

Wild fruit (owner, 2026-11-15: "fruits to be found in the wild ... mango, kiwi, regional fruits"): fruit hangs in the crowns of some of a biome's trees, with a few fallen under them, and forage plants grow in patches. Temperate: apple, pear, plum and cherry on the oaks; wild strawberry, blackberry, raspberry, blueberry, tomato and kiwi vines. Jungle: mango and papaya on the kapoks, banana and coconut on the palms; watermelon and tomato. Savanna: baobab fruit and marula on the acacias; wild watermelon. Taiga: pine nuts in cones; lingonberry, cloudberry and blueberry. Desert: dates on the oasis palms; prickly pear. Swamp: cranberry and elderberry. Fungal forest: glowfruit under the glowcaps and on stalks. Crystal flora: gem berries. Kelp drift: bladder pods. Drawn by foliage/fruit_art.py without outlines; leaves and vines are recoloured to the biome's ground ramp as the tufts are, the fruit keeps its own colour so it can be spotted. Fruit thins out in winter and is heaviest in autumn.

The painted tree impostors are only a placeholder for the moment before the bake below finishes. A card's leaf, needle, frond or blade shape is cut from the game tile by a pixel mask in the shader, so no leaf texture is drawn at all.

Mid and far trees are baked from the near tree. Once a biome's textures have loaded, every tree variant's real near model (its branches, needle sprays and game textures, in no season, bare in winter; autumn and spring are laid on live) is rendered orthographically from 8 bearings into a strip of 48 x 72 px views, read back, snapped to the Sweaft-64 palette with alpha 0 or 255, and given to that variant's mid cards (four crossed planes, each showing the view baked from its own side) and far billboard (the view nearest the camera's bearing). Near, mid and far are one tree, so the cross-fade between them never changes what the tree is.

Fallen leaves (owner, 2026-11-15: "autumn ground should have some leaf coverage, maybe use shader for this so we can kind of customize it", "and show gradually more leaves"). Drawn by the terrain shader on what faces up, in the ground tile's own texels: a leaf is a blob of two or three texels, fixed to its cell, never swimming. Each slot of about two texels holds two leaves, each with its own moment in the fall, so they land one by one as Fallen leaves rises; under a crown more have landed (Gather under trees). Their colours are fallen leaves' (rust, ochre, oak brown, tan, old gold), lit and shaded as the ground is, with 12% of the ground's own colour mixed in. Autumn shows the slider's fall times the biome's broadleaf share; winter keeps 60% of it, browned, frosted, lying on the snow; spring and summer have none. "Let them fall" runs the fall from bare ground to covered over 40 s; it is off until pressed.

No painted flowers on the ground (owner, 2026-11-15: "I would also remove the flowers from the ground texture as well, we have actual flowers already"). foliage/no_flowers.py takes the daisies off the fields grass top and sod lip (temperate), the pale sprouts off the swamp moss, and the orange flower dots off the tundra top (the taiga's ground), in this mockup's copy of the atlas only. The jungle's yellow fern sprigs are leaves and stay.

Palettes that fit the place. Where a game tile does not suit the biome it stands in, it is drawn through a three-colour gradient map of its own luminance (RAMPS), so it keeps its pixel pattern and wears the place's colours: the savanna's acacias and bushes use the game's leaves in a dry olive (the pale grey-white sage they wore read as frost); in the fungal forest the sporewood cap skin is the icon's violet, the stalks a darker warm cream, and the pale mint moss a dark violet moss, under a darker sky, so the cyan glow reads at night. The tufts and the fruit plants' leaves take the same ground palette.

Fruit hangs on wood. Each tree model records the points fruit may hang from (a pine's branches part way to their tips, an oak's twigs, an acacia's crown twigs, a palm's head, a glowcap's gills); a fruit card hangs down from one of them, turned and scaled with its tree (and with Tree scale), so no cone or fruit floats beside the crown.

What made the far trees' colour fragile, and what holds it now. The baked impostors were made in whatever season was showing, and only a rebuild re-bakes; switching from autumn back to summer does not rebuild, so a forest baked in autumn (after a biome change, Let them fall, or a page left in autumn while textures loaded) kept peach-brown far trees in summer. That is not one browser's: it reproduces in Chromium. The bake is now seasonless (autumn and spring are laid on live, as on the near leaves). The other read-backs were browser-dependent: the means and tuft ramps came from getImageData and the bake from readPixels, which Firefox with resistFingerprinting (and Tor Browser) answers with white or random pixels, and its fingerprinting protection jitters. Both are now tested once on a known pattern; if the read-back is not trustworthy the plants keep their full texture, the tufts a painted ramp, and the trees the painted impostors. The 2D canvases ask for sRGB explicitly; the PNGs carry no colour chunks and three.js r128 uploads with colour-space conversion off and no premultiply, so nothing else is converted. A mean that lands after a bake re-bakes the trees that use it.

Leaf cards hang from their branches. Every leaf, needle, frond and willow card has its root edge on the branch it grows from and points out along it. A wind mask, zero at the root and one at the tip (the square of the distance along the card), scales the card's own flutter, so the root stays pinned while the tree's sway moves branch and card together. "Show wind mask" colours cards black at the root and white at the tip.

How it stays consistent with the plans

  • No pop-in (priority 1). Every layer switches level with a screen-door Bayer 4 x 4 dither in which the outgoing level keeps exactly the pixels the incoming one gives up, so there is never a frame where both or neither show. By default the switch is timed: when a plant crosses a ring (with 4% hysteresis) its two levels cross-fade over the fade time, then the new level is solid, so a dither is only ever on screen while a fade is in progress. Changing a setting fades the new plants in the same way. "Distance cross-fade band" is the distance-lod-fade design (a standing dither in a band of width "fade width" times the ring, the shipped lod_fade_width 0.3), kept to compare; "Hard cut" shows the pop both replace. Fly-by shows them in motion.
  • Trees stand one per cell, at the cell centre, as Tenebris places them, at the biome rates of tenebris-tree-geometry scaled by the slider. The hex-prism style is that change's geometry: a 0.20 trunk, two leaf layers at 0.65 + 0.35 x hash of the cell, a pine as one wood block under a cone of layers 0.95 down to 0.15, never rotated off the grid.
  • Grass blades take their colour from a narrow slice of the ground tile they grow on, as Tenebris's scatter does; the mid ring swaps to a crossed card cut from the same tile, and past that the ground tile carries the colour.
  • Biomes are big (bigger-biomes): one patch is one biome, about a tenth of a 750 m biome's width.
  • Lit at night. Lanterns along the path, a campfire, glowing fungi, crystals and fireflies each light what is near them wherever the camera is. The static lights are baked into each terrain vertex and each plant, as the towns mockup bakes block light; the 16 fireflies move, so they are summed in the shader, all of them, every frame. The moon is kept dim so the lamps read.

What it leaves out

  • Distances are scaled to the patch. The game's rings are hundreds of metres (trees fade out at about 1.2 to 2.4 km); here they are tens, so the whole ladder fits on screen.
  • No shadow maps. Trees darken the ground and plants under their crowns with a baked occlusion term instead.
  • Changing a density or the biome rebuilds the patch; the new plants fade in rather than appear. In the game the scatter is a pure function of the cell, so it does not change in play.
  • Frame time here is a browser's, not the engine's. The counters are for comparing settings against each other, not for budgets.

Glow and carried light

The owner, 2026-11-19: "Players also asked to pick up glowing mushrooms. You can eat them and glow light from your body temporarily", "Allow players to pick up the glowing mushrooms that exist in the game today", "Holding a light source, torches lamps or mushrooms or anything that is a light should illuminate surrounding area", and "Need to make this fast so this would be realtime for the light emanating from player". The write-up is openspec/changes/glowing-mushrooms. Nothing on this tab is in the game yet.

Controls

  • Drag to look, WASD or the arrow keys to walk, or Walk the route to be walked past the mushrooms and through the doorway in the stone wall.
  • F (or the Pick button) picks the glowing mushroom under the crosshair, within 5 m. A toast shows its icon, its name and how many.
  • 1-4 or a tap selects a hotbar slot: torch, lantern, glowing mushroom, bare hand.
  • Right click (or Eat) with the mushroom selected eats one. The buff over the hotbar counts down. Glow time ×10 runs the clock ten times faster to see the fade.
  • Light: Today is the game now: nothing carried lights anything. Carried is the proposal.
  • Overview shows the player from above, so the pool of light and where the wall cuts it can be seen.

The plant is the game's own

It is the glowing plant of lamps-and-lanterns decision 8, drawn as planet_surface.wgsl's flower slot draws it: a 6 cm stalk 0.32 m tall and a low four-sided cap 14 cm across, 3 cm high. It stands at a corner share of its cell from the cell's own rolls. At night its cap burns in its own cyan, (0.62, 1.0, 0.88) × 0.25, and it lights its own cell on the field at level 3, which the field draws in its one warm tint. By day the cap is pale. The game grows one in a hundred green cells (glow_flower_chance 0.01); this patch has more so that a few are in view. The code calls it a flower, and the owner calls it a mushroom. Cap: Proposed is the recommended look change, an eight-sided dome 16 cm across, 6 cm high and darker underneath. It goes to the survey with screenshots, and the game keeps today's cap until the owner answers.

Picking, saving, regrowth

  • F aims at the plant itself (as fruit-aim does), within 0.25 m of its stalk or cap.
  • A pick gives one glowing mushroom and writes a glow-plant record by the cell's exact key. A picked cell draws no plant, and no other flower, and gives no light.
  • It grows back after 8 world days, but only when its cell is rebuilt more than 120 m from the camera. It never appears in front of the player.
  • No generator version: where they grow is a function of the cell key and the config chance, which a digest test pins. A world nobody picked from saves nothing new.

Eating and the body glow

  • Right click eats one, as a dish is eaten. It runs Glow for 3 minutes of play. There is no hunger.
  • Eating another while it runs sets the time back to the full 3 minutes. It never stacks, and it is never brighter: cooking's rule for every effect.
  • The glow is a level 10 light (between a candle's 8 and a hanging lantern's 12) in the cap's cyan, at the chest. It rises over 0.6 s and falls on a smoothstep over the last 20 s, so it dims and goes out and never cuts.

One carried light

  • Whatever is in the selected slot and gives light when placed gives the same light in hand: a torch at level 14, a lantern at 13, the mushroom at 3, all in the field's warm tint. The values are Material::emission and GLOW_FLOWER_LEVEL, never a copy. A lantern and a mushroom light from dusk to dawn, as they do when placed. In the pack, a light gives nothing.
  • The strength is level / 14, and the falloff is the lamp bake's own: N·L × w² / (1 + (d/2.2)²) × 2.4 with w = 1 − (d/16)⁴. So a held torch lights the ground as a placed torch at the same distance does.
  • The body glow and a held light add: one light per player, at the stronger source, its colour the sum.
  • It is worked out every frame from the exact position, so it never lags and never steps from cell to cell. Its shadow is a walk through the columns from each pixel toward the light, stopped by the first solid cell. Walk up to the stone wall: the ground on the far side stays dark, and the doorway lets the light through.
  • It lights the ground, the trees, the plants, the dropped log (a 2D item, lit like the ground), and the hand, which takes it unshadowed and no nearer than 0.5 m.

In the engine

The recommended way is a point light evaluated in the terrain, field-lit and water shaders beside the baked field, with the column walk for its shadow. That is about 0.3-0.5 ms on the RTX 3070 at 1080p and 0.3-0.6 ms on the M4 Max, estimated from L8's measured voxel walk. A light volume on the GPU would be cheaper but blockier and leaky, and re-baking the field on the CPU would step and lag. The owner measures it with perf_suite.py's night walk with a torch, A/B in one sitting. This page draws the same terms in WebGL on hex columns. The sun and moon use the same walk for their shadows here, which the game does with its cascades instead.
