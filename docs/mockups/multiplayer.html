Multiplayer: host, join, another player, your character

The mockups of openspec/changes/multiplayer tasks 5.1 and 5.2 and of openspec/changes/player-character task 1.1, for the owner's approval before any game UI or body is built. Four tabs, each a bare link: Host (#host), Join (#join), Player (#player) and Character (#character). Player and Character draw 3D, one view each; a view is made when its tab first opens and stops drawing when its tab is closed.

Versions

  • 1 (2026-11-27): Host, Join and Player.
  • 2 (2026-11-27): the other player is M5's townsperson, the game's NPC figure (owner: "humans should be consistent with the NPCs in the game").
  • 3 (2026-11-27): the Character tab (player-character 1.1): the character screen as a tab of the Options menu, and the same figure in a rowing boat, a hand cart, on a horse, in a car and in a plane. The title menu becomes CONTINUE, NEW WORLD, MULTIPLAYER, OPTIONS, QUIT, with Continue's list of worlds and a Servers list on the Join panel.

Host (task 5.1, design 14.3)

  • The pause menu as the game draws it today (desktop/menu.rs: RESUME, SAVES, SETTINGS, MAIN MENU, QUIT under the PAUSED lintel) gains HOST, second. Today hosting is --host <port> on the command line (design 20); this is the screen that replaces it.
  • Before hosting: a port (47123, the one the two-machine test used), an optional password, and SHOW ON LAN, which makes the game appear in other players' On LAN list. A port outside 1024 to 65535 is refused on the panel; Taken in the bar plays a port already in use.
  • While hosting: the address others join by (this machine's LAN address and the port) with COPY, and who is connected, up to 8 (design 14.2). Each guest has KICK and BAN; BAN asks first. STOP asks first when someone is on. The world keeps going under the menu; RESUME goes back to play while still hosting.
  • Joins and leaves show as a line over the hotbar's place, the way the game's toasts read. Use A player joins and A player leaves in the bar.
  • No relay and no public address yet (design 20: a direct address or the LAN among friends); the address shown is the LAN one.

Join (task 5.1, design 9)

  • The title menu (version 3, the owner): five entries, CONTINUE, NEW WORLD, MULTIPLAYER, OPTIONS, QUIT. Today's (menu.rs) is CONTINUE, NEW WORLD, LOAD WORLD, SETTINGS, QUIT: LOAD WORLD folds into CONTINUE, SETTINGS is renamed OPTIONS, and MULTIPLAYER is new. Joining leaves no world of yours open, so it is on the title, not the pause menu; hosting stays on the pause menu.
  • Continue opens the list of saved worlds, latest first, with LOAD LATEST at the top; LOAD on a row loads that one. Either goes to the loading screen.
  • Multiplayer opens the Join panel: your NAME (sent in the Hello), an ADDRESS with its port, SERVERS (a server someone keeps running, a hosted game that is always up, kept once joined; its address may be a name rather than a LAN address) and ON LAN, the games hosts on this network show. A game with a password shows a lock and asks for it; a full one shows FULL and cannot be pressed.
  • Options opens the settings screen, now with a CHARACTER tab: the Character tab of this page.
  • The host answers in the bar picks what the host says to the next JOIN, so every message can be seen. The messages are short and plain, one line each:
The protocol's answerThe player reads
JoinRefusal::GameVersionGame version differs
JoinRefusal::WorldVersionWorld version differs
JoinRefusal::DifferentWorldDifferent world
JoinRefusal::FullGame is full
JoinRefusal::BannedBanned from this game
JoinRefusal::WrongPasswordWrong password (the password field again)
No answer (the connect times out; not a refusal)No game at that address
  • "Game version differs" and "Different world" are design 9's words. With the launcher (game-launcher design 8) the first may grow a second line telling the player to restart it; that waits for the launcher.
  • A Welcome goes to the loading screen while the world's regions arrive (design 9: it stays up until they and every pipeline are ready).

Player (task 5.2, design 14.5)

  • A patch of the fields on hex columns (2.833 m across, 1 m layers) with the game's terrain tiles, a packed path, the game's trees and lamp posts. You stand at the near end; Wren walks the path. Close, Near, Middle, Far and the slider send Wren to that distance; Wren walks there and stops, then turns to face you. Nothing moves until you ask.
  • The name over them only when near: the name is drawn in the kit's font over the head, at the UI's whole-number scale, within 24 m, and not past it (recommendation taken, ask only with screenshots: about eight cells, where a face can still be told apart). It shows and hides in a fifth of a second as the line is crossed, and does not linger.
  • The same figure as the game's townsfolk (version 2, owner, 2026-11-27: "humans should be consistent with the NPCs in the game"): another player is M5's townsperson, the figure the game draws for its NPCs, built by the same kit (multiplayer/figure-kit.js, copied from npcs.html) and rolled from the Fields folk's palette. In the game another player is drawn by the townsfolk's own code (multiplayer design 24), so the two cannot drift apart.
  • Lit like the ground: the terrain's sun with its shadows, and at night the lamps' light per pixel with the towns mockup's falloff (1 - (d/R)^2)^2, every lamp lighting what is near it wherever the camera is. The moon is held at 0.035, so outside the pools the ground and Wren read near black: in the game that is LitByField and LitLikeTerrain on the body's root.
  • Eyes is your own view (drag to look, arrow keys too). Orbit Wren turns about the body to see it close (drag, wheel or pinch to zoom).

Character (player-character task 1.1)

  • Why. The owner, 2026-11-27: "Would be nice to be able to customize character for single player too. This would allow me to have an in game model dude for third person vehicle views like the boat rowing, car driving plane flying stuff and horse riding", and "humans should be consistent with the NPCs in the game". Today the player has no body: first person shows only the hands, the rower in the boat's chase view is a stand-in, and the cart, horse, car and plane show nobody at the controls.
  • Where it is made: a CHARACTER tab of the Options menu (the settings screen's PICTURE, SOUND, INPUT, MOVEMENT, WEATHER and WORLD tabs, with this one added), so the look can be changed at any time, from the title or the pause menu (owner, 2026-11-27: fully customizable from Options at any time). A new world opens it once before it loads.
  • One figure, one art (design 1): the body is M5's townsperson, the figure the game draws for its NPCs (pbd_core::townsfolk::look::Look and figure; here multiplayer/figure-kit.js). No other body is made, and a choice the townsfolk's parts cannot draw is not offered.
  • The choices, every one on screen at once: Build (round, pear, tall, stout), Height (within the build's range, as the townsfolk roll it), Skin, Hair (short, bun, braid, bald) and its colour, Beard (none, goatee, full), Face (the resting face: happy, calm, thinking, sleepy, the four a townsperson rests in), Shirt, Trousers, Shoes, and Hat (none, a brim hat, a cap, a hood, a wrap, a helmet or a headset) with its colour. Under a hat the hair shows at the back only, as on the townsfolk; a hood or a headset covers the ears.
  • Swatches, not a colour picker (design 3): skin, hair and cloth come from the peoples' palettes (look::People; here multiplayer/peoples.js, copied from npcs.html), across every people, so a character always sits in the game's palette. Shoes are the townsfolk's three; a hat's colours are the cloth and the role hats' own (straw, helmet iron, headset grey).
  • Random rolls a look as look::generate rolls a townsperson: a people at random, its palette, and a hat from that people's hats.
  • Seen in (design 4 and 5): the same figure, from outside, in the chase view of each craft. Each craft names a seat and where the hands go; the arms are aimed there from the shoulders and the controls meet the hands, whatever the build and height:
    • Rowing boat: seated on the thwart facing the stern, the hands on the oar handles, the oars through the oarlocks;
    • Hand cart: standing between the shafts, a hand on each;
    • Horse: astride the saddle, the hands on the reins;
    • Car: seated at the wheel, the hands on its rim;
    • Plane: seated in the open cockpit, the hands on the stick.
    First person stays hands only. Drag (or the arrow keys) turns the view about the figure; the wheel or a pinch zooms. Nothing turns on its own.
  • Lit as the ground is: the terrain's sun with its shadows by day; at night the moon is held at 0.035 and every lamp lights what is near it, per pixel, with the towns mockup's falloff: the lamp posts by the path and the field, the boat's stern lantern, the car's headlamps and the plane's panel lamp. In the game that is LitByField and LitLikeTerrain on the figure's root.
  • Where it is kept (design 2): one look per install, saves/character.ron beside install.key, so every world and every host sees the same person; until one is made, a look is rolled from the install's key. Nothing in a save changes. In multiplayer Hello carries the look and other players are drawn with it (design 6, a protocol version).
  • Open, recommendations taken (ask only with screenshots):
    • Where the screen opens on a new world: before the world loads (recommended), or on first spawn.
    • Whether a hat is worn in a vehicle: yes, unless it is a helmet a closed cockpit would clip (recommended). The craft here are all open, so every hat stays on.
  • The craft are built for this mockup as lofted, smooth-shaded forms, textured with the game's own settlement set (plank, boards, timber, iron, hide, linen, still_water) and the herd's horse hide (herd_hide, row 0). The car's paint and the tyres' rubber are 32 px textures painted in the page, in the same palette; a game version would draw them with a script in tools/art/. They stand in for the craft's own models (M38, M23, M24), which are not changed here.

Which art is whose

  • The game's: the UI kit's oak frame, plaques, tabs and stone lintel, slot well, check, bar and tooltip (assets/ui/, the smooth renders, sampled linear as the game samples the kit); the kit font (assets/fonts/kit-font.json) and VT323 for body lines; the terrain atlas (assets/tilesets/atlas.png); the timber and iron of the lamp posts (assets/textures/settlement/). The backdrops are game captures (docs/mockups/ui/game/). Provenance: docs/mockups/multiplayer/README.md.
  • Mockup-only: the figure's tiles are the townsfolk mockup's (tools/art/npcs_art.py); the title backdrop is a still of the UI mockup's own demo.
