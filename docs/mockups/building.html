Pale Blue Dot · design mockup · Proposal, not built

Building on the hex grid

All Pale Blue Dot mockups (the tracker)

Walls on cell edges, floors in cells, a storey every three layers: the town's own cut, put in the player's hands. Pick a piece, aim at the plot, click. The ghost is green when the town's rules accept it and red with the reason when they do not. It is night, so what you build is lit by the lanterns you place.

This is the build mockup openspec/changes/player-building asks for first (it names it build.html), grown from the towns mockup's cut and checks, on a plot of 91 cells. It runs on dig-and-place's journal and lamps-and-lanterns' lights. Everything here is proposed, not built behaviour. Walls, roofs, floors and ground are the game's own textures: the settlement set (assets/textures/settlement) at the manifest's scale, and the terrain atlas's own tiles on hex columns 1 m a layer (the fields sheet's sward, sward-over-earth and earth; the lunar regolith sheet on the moon), with the game's trees, all sampled nearest. The hotbar is the game's (desktop/slots.rs: ten 44 px slots), its lantern the game's own icon (assets/items/lights), removing the game's pickaxe. Mockup-only, because the game has none yet: the habitat's metal panel and glass, and the piece icons, all pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit, the solitaire collection's style (tools/art/building_art.py): the icons 32 px, the metal and glass 32 px tiles like the game's settlement textures, all shown at whole-number scales and sampled nearest.

Lit at night as CLAUDE.md asks: every lantern you place, the town's two street lamps and each window with a lantern behind it light what is near them, wherever the camera is, each throwing a pool on the ground as the game's lamps do; the moon stays at 0.035, as dark as the game's night, so the ground between the pools reads near black. Placing or removing anything re-lights, as an edit re-lights the field in the engine. Building is free (owner, P1); costs come later and are sketched in section 3.

2 · Build pieces

What snaps where

Every piece lands on the cut, so a player's house and a town's house are the same kind of thing and the walker, collision and light treat them alike. Sizes are the engine's: a cell is 2.833 m flat to flat, its edge 1.636 m, a layer 1 m.

3 · Materials per tier

From reeds to pressure hulls

A tier is a kit: the same pieces, cut the same way, made of something better. Each tier's materials come from the production chain in game-design section 10, so a tier is reached by going somewhere, not by grinding a number.

4 · Progression

Hut, stone house, habitat

A reed hut on the first night

One cell, six reed walls 2.2 m high, a door, a thatch cone. Cut reeds and a few poles; an axe and your hands. It keeps the rain off and holds a lantern. Load "Hut" above.

A stone house in a town

Three cells, half-metre fieldstone walls, windows on a 1 m sill, a gable roof, lanterns at the door and inside, so its windows light the street. Built beside the town, it is yours; the town grows round it, never over it. Load "Stone house".

A habitat on an airless moon

Seven cells of metal panel and glass under a flat roof, one airlock. The builder checks the seal: every outside edge walled, every cell floored and roofed. Leave an edge open and it names the leak in red. Load "Habitat" with the moon as the site.

6 · The world never overwrites the player

What the town may do with your house

Towns will grow on their own (owner, 2026-09-27). Every change goes through the same durable journal and names its author, and world-persistence says a world-authored change that would alter a cell or piece the player has changed is refused. Press "Let the town grow" with something built: the town lays a fence round the plot's edge and stops at every cell you touched, and the journal shows each refusal.

7 · Decisions / questions for the owner

What needs your call

B1 · Aim-snap or grid cursor?

The ghost here follows the mouse to the nearest slot, the way blocks are placed today.

Recommendation: aim-snap on foot, as player-building decision 2 says; the top-down view in this page is the mockup's convenience, not a mode.

B2 · Does the moon habitat need air to be a gameplay rule?

The seal check shows pressurised or leaking. It could also be what lets you take the helmet off.

Recommendation: yes, later: a sealed habitat is a breathable room. For now the check is shown, with no survival rule behind it.

B3 · Tiers as kits, with materials from places

Section 3 ties each tier to a body: timber and stone on Tenebris, resin on Sequoia, alloys from Crag, seals from Frostbite.

Recommendation: yes, once costs exist (P1 put costs after the rest of the roadmap). Until then every kit is free.

B4 · Upper storeys and the stair

A straight flight takes two cells and needs the floor above cut away, as towns do it.

Recommendation: keep the towns' three stairs only; no ladders, so every house walks.

B5 · The page name

player-building calls this mockup build.html; this one is building.html.

Recommendation: rename the change's reference to building.html when this is approved.
