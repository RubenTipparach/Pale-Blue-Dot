About

Phase 5's land transport, on the roads phase 3 builds. The owner, 2026-11-21: add cars and cars and carts, and horses and trains (and tracks) mockups to phase 5, and 2026-11-22: Roads and cars mockup?. This is design only: nothing here is in the game, and phase 5 does not start until the owner asks. The OpenSpec change is land-transport.

The roads are townsfolk's road cells, cut the way towns.html's Roads scene cuts them: a cell is 2.833 m flat to flat, a layer is 1 m, and a road cell that climbs slopes its top as one plane from the middle of the edge it is entered by to the middle of the edge it leaves by, one layer over the cell (19°). Dirt and gravel use the same pixel textures as that scene; the bridge is its timber deck on posts.

Nothing moves until you press Drive. Drag to turn the view, scroll or pinch to come closer, right-drag or two fingers to slide it. Follow keeps a vehicle in the middle without turning the view. Drive line draws the line the wheels follow. Night (or the time slider) lights each scene by its own lamps: posts by the road, the lanterns on the carts, the cars' headlamps, the engine's lamp, the coach's windows and the station's lamps; the moon is dim. The open tab is in the link (#carts, #cars, #trains, #side); only its view is made and drawn.

Carts and horses

  • A road loop of 40 cells: a level dirt track in the south, a climb of three layers up each end (three sloped cells, a layer a cell), and a gravel road over the hilltop with a timber bridge over a ravine.
  • A saddled horse with a rider, a horse in the shafts of a two-wheeled cart, and a horse on the pole of a four-wheeled covered wagon. A hitching rail at the west end with a horse tied to it and a lamp over it.
  • A cart's two wheels pitch it to its horse: the cart points from its axle to the collar, so on a climb it tilts with the horse. A wagon's front axle turns on its kingpin to follow the pole, and the body sits on both axles.
  • Horses walk at about 1.6 m/s and slow on a climb. A horse's legs move only while it walks; a parked cart's wheels stand still.
  • Every vehicle fits in one road cell, so two cannot pass on a one-cell road. Recommendation taken (ask only with screenshots): roads stay one cell wide, with a two-cell passing place every so often; the world's roads between towns get one at each lamp.

Cars

  • The same loop. An early open tourer (wheelbase 2.4 m) and a small flatbed truck with crates (wheelbase 3.0 m), both on wooden-spoked or steel disc wheels.
  • The bend. A road bends 60° in one cell, never sharper. A vehicle drives it as an arc from the middle of the cell before the bend to the middle of the cell after it: a radius of 4.9 m (twice the cell's 2.45 m in-cell arc), which an early car can turn. The arc passes 0.76 m inside the bend cell's centre and stays on the road cells; a 1.6 m car's inside wheel crosses the cell's inner edge by about 0.1 m, so a bend's inside neighbour must be free of walls and fences to wheel height. Two bends in neighbouring cells halve the arc (2.45 m), which carts and cars can take at walking pace. A 120° turn in one cell is not a road; it needs two bends.
  • The climb. One layer a cell is 19° (a 35% grade). A horse walks it; an early car climbs it in low gear at about 60% of its speed. Steeper than a layer a cell is never a road.
  • The bridge. The timber deck is the road at its own height, so a car crosses it as it crosses gravel. Recommendation taken (ask only with screenshots): bridges carry everything that fits on the road; there is no weight limit in phase 5.
  • Headlamps throw their light ahead of the car and light the road and the verge there; a lamp post lights the car as it passes.

Trains and tracks

  • Straight track runs from cell centre to cell centre along one of the six hex directions, 2.833 m a cell, so every straight lies on the grid.
  • A curve turns 60° and starts and ends on cell centres: it goes two cells along the old heading and two along the new one, which makes a circle of radius 2·√3·2.833 = 9.81 m. Six curves close a loop. A bigger curve piece (three and three, 14.7 m) is the same rule for faster lines. A curve does not lie on cell centres between its ends; its cells are the cells its bed covers.
  • A grade. A train cannot climb a layer a cell. The loop climbs one layer over six cells (1 in 17, 5.9%) on its south straight and comes down the same on the north. Half the grade runs on an embankment over the low ground and half in a cutting through the terrace.
  • The bed. Track is laid on a ballast bed whose top follows the grade. Cells taller than the bed are cut down to the layer under it, and cells lower than that layer are filled up to it; the bed's sloped bank makes up the part of a layer between, as a road cell's wedge does. So the world keeps whole cells, and only the bed is sloped.
  • The station. A platform is a row of cells one layer above the bed, faced in fieldstone with a timber edge that reaches to within 0.2 m of the train. A small booking hut, a bench and two lamps.
  • A little tank engine (metre gauge, 5.1 m) with a coach and an open wagon, 15 m in all. The coach's windows are lit at night and light the platform beside it.

  • The town tree's Carts branch (M33's Town tab) walks a player through it: wheels, a hand cart, haul a load, buy a horse, hitch up, 500 m on the road.

In the engine

  • A road vehicle reads only the road cells: their top (flat, or the slope's plane), whether a cell is a bridge deck, and the cell's surface (dirt, gravel) for its speed and sound. It never needs a GPU readback.
  • A vehicle is a stored record like a boat: its ID, its pose in the body's frame (a WorldPos, f64), its cargo, damage and owner, and the animal in its shafts by that animal's own ID. A train is an ordered list of its cars' IDs and where it stands on its track. Track and its bed are stored edits on the durable path, made by the player like a road.
  • Vehicles in the world get LitByField and LitLikeTerrain, so a cart in a cutting at night is as dark as the cutting, and its lantern lights it.
