Survival

The owner, 2026-11-26: "start working on phase 4, we're gonna work on survival mode which will add new branch to starter missions to handle survival." This page draws openspec/changes/survival-mode (decisions 9 and 12 to 18) and openspec/changes/wildfire. Written up, not built: nothing here is in the game yet. Five tabs, each a bare #anchor; one 3D view at a time (Fire, Weapons), made when its tab first opens.

HUD: Explorer beside Survival

The same view twice: on the left an Explorer world (today's calm game: the hotbar and the compass, nothing else), on the right a Survival world. Day and Night: at night the scene is lit by its own campfire and torch and a dim moon.

  • Four short bars above the hotbar, Survival only: health (red), stamina (yellow), hunger (orange), water (blue). Hatching on the stamina bar is the strain still owed: points that cost half as much again in food to refill (design 12).
  • A thermometer at their end with the body's temperature, a trend arrow and the cause (sun, snow, fire, water) beside it (design 13).
  • A flame beside the thermometer where it is dry enough to burn (wildfire design 2), no text.
  • Breath shows only under water, as bubbles (design 5).
  • States are the bars' and the screen's, never a line of text: winded flashes the empty stamina bar; heat warms the screen's edge, and heat stroke fades it to dark; cold frosts it; a worn tool has a thin bar under its slot, red below a tenth, and a broken one is cracked and dim (design 11). The one line is "You died" (design 9).
  • Bars and slots are the game's stone-and-carved-wood kit (assets/ui, as M33). Item icons are the game's where it has them; the spear, hide, meat and marks are stand-ins (tools/mockup_survival_icons.py).
  • The scene is a flat pixel painting standing in for the game's view, lit per pixel by every light in it, so the HUD can be judged by day and at night.

Stamina and food

The owner: "Stamina regen relies on food levels. It'll consume stored energy when it regens. Half stamina consumes less food. Pushing or exerting yourself further, the stamina bar will decrease slowly but also consume food at the same time and consume more food to regen back up."

The graph runs survival-mode design 12 in the page, the same model as tools/survival/stamina_model.py (10 ms steps). Every slider is a number of the proposed survival.ron; move one and the curve, the totals and the cost-per-point chart redraw.

  • Spend: sprint and swim drain the bar slowly and burn food while they do (strain); a jump and a climbed metre spend a little and leave no strain. Tool work spends nothing.
  • Refill: after the delay, at the rate, each point costing food at the bar's height: the low cost up to half, rising in a straight line to the high cost at full (the lower chart). Strain multiplies it.
  • Food sets the rate: full at 50 food or more, down to 40% at none.

A small grass fire at night in the dry season

The owner: "Survival: you can start foresr fires during dry seasons. Torches can light up stuff or can be out out by rain." And on the first draft, 2026-11-26: "Let's not spread fire too much for now , consider lots of factors like dryness of plants" (wildfire decision 12, with Our World in Data's Wildfires page as ideas: grassland burns far more than forest, each hemisphere burns in its own summer, farmed and grazed land breaks the fuel up). This tab runs wildfire design 2, 3, 5 and 7 on a patch of hex cells (2.83 m, 1 m layers): grass, shrubs, broadleaf and conifer trees, a stream, a dug break of bare soil, a pen's grazed pasture and a player's tilled field.

  • Every plant has a dryness, 0 to 1, from its kind (grass cures to straw, a shrub less, a tree much less, a cactus never), the season, how long since rain, the air's humidity and warmth, shade under trees, water nearby (the stream's banks stay green), hollows and rises, the soil's own patches, and grazing (round the pen). Dryness shows it: green is wetter, yellow to orange will burn.
  • Green plants do not burn: below 0.5 nothing catches, and above it the chance grows as the square of the margin. Spring: nothing catches anywhere, a torch makes a 3 s flame. Summer: still too green. Dry season (late summer after two dry weeks): the open grass burns, the banks, the wood's floor and the pasture do not.
  • The rule: every second each burning cell tries each of its six neighbours that is dry enough, at a hazard of the fuel's spread x its dryness x the wind (faster downwind, slower up it) x the slope x the rain. Grass burns 15 s, a shrub 40 s, a tree 90 s at its foot; only a very dry conifer in a wind of 6 m/s or more burns in its crown. No embers are thrown.
  • It stays small: a front runs about 0.2 m/s, goes out where it meets green or wetter ground, and is held to 300 cells (about 50 m across) and 40 burning at once.
  • It stops at water, the dug break, green ground and the player's field (a change the world makes never touches the player's), and in rain: slide Rain up and the front slows, then goes out.
  • It leaves ash, scorched trees and, after a crown fire, charred snags. "Day after" shows the scar by daylight; the game grows it back over 60 world days.
  • Lit by its own fire: every burning cell lights the ground and trees near it (grouped into up to 48 lights), under a dim moon. Flames, smoke and embers are particles.
  • Shown at six seconds of fire a real second; Pause stops it. Click a cell to light it with a torch.
  • Trees here are this page's stand-ins for the game's tree draw, shaped and textured, not the game's own models.
