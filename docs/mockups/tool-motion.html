Tools in Hand

All Pale Blue Dot mockups (the tracker)

About this mockup

Mockup M36, the tool in hand: digging, chopping and fishing in first person. The owner, 2026-11-17: "The dudes hand kinda of has weird normals. Also the fishing rod isnt animated when casting or when a fish is bighting let's fix that up. Make a new fishing mockup", then "make a new mockup for all the tools stuff for digging, choppin and fishing", after "man that axe swing is weak, it should be more visceral, more faster". Plans: openspec/changes/held-swing (dig and chop) and openspec/changes/rod-motion (the rod and the hand).

Each tab has one 3D view and a Today / Proposed switch in it. Day / Night at the top applies to every tab. Nothing moves until you hold the mouse on the view or press a button; only the world moves on its own (water, the float on it, a lantern's flame).

Dig

  • The pickaxe on a stone cell, or the shovel on a grass cell (the picker). Hold the mouse on the view, or press Dig, to dig the cell ahead. Cracks grow with the dig's progress, the cell breaks and pops away, and a fresh one comes back after 0.15 s (between_s, dig.ron).
  • The break times are the game's, assets/config/dig.ron: the stone tier's matrix (pickaxe on stone 1.2 s, rock 1.6 s, ore 2.0 s; shovel on dirt 0.5 s; axe on wood 0.6 s) divided by the tier's speed (1, 1.6, 2.4, 3.6).
  • Today is the game's chop (held::hold): a sine at 9 rad/s, chop = sin(phase).max(-0.2), pitching the tool 0.85 rad forward about the fist and dropping it 6 cm; it has no hits of its own, the block breaks when the time is up, and letting go snaps the tool to rest.
  • Proposed is the STRIKE stroke of held-swing: a wind-up of 0.08 s back to −0.35 rad, the strike in 0.07 s to +1.15 rad eased in with t³ (fastest at impact), a 0.05 s hold on impact (the tool only; the dig runs on), a 0.12 rad kick back and 0.18 s back to rest. The fist drops 9 cm in step with the angle. A block of break time T gets N = max(1, round(T / 0.4)) strokes, the k-th impact at progress k/N, the last on the break. Letting go eases the tool back over recover_s. The camera kick is 0 unless its slider is raised.

Chop

  • The axe on a tree trunk, the same two strokes as Dig. Wood with the stone axe is 0.6 s, which is 2 strokes (round(1.5)); an iron axe's 0.25 s is one.

Fish

  • Hold the mouse on the view to charge, let go to cast, as in the game (power over 1.2 s; the float flies at 5–18 m/s with 3–6 m/s of lift, fauna.ron). Click while the float is under to strike; hold to reel while a fish is on; right click winds the line in.
  • The buttons play one phase each: Charge, Cast, Nibble, Bite, Fight (the fish stays on until another button; Reel turns the crank, Tension sets the line's tension) and Catch. Play all runs the whole cast to a catch once.
  • Today: the rod stands still in every phase; only the float and the line change, and the line leaves the rod's rest tip.
  • Proposed (rod-motion design 2): a pitch about the fist (tip up and back positive), a roll and a shift of the fist, driven by a damped spring toward each phase's target (spring_hz 9, damping 0.7), so a phase change never pops the rod. Charging draws back to +draw_rad with the charge, eased, the fist up and back draw_lift_m; the cast flicks to −flick_rad in flick_s and springs to the fishing pose −settle_rad with a slow bob; a nibble twitches the tip nibble_rad for nibble_s; a bite dips it to −bite_rad at once and shakes at 14 Hz for the window; a fish on lifts it to +fight_rad, shaking fight_shake_rad × tension at 9 Hz, rolling toward the fish's pull up to roll_rad, and while reeling the fist turns a crank_m circle at 3 Hz; a catch lifts +catch_rad and eases back over catch_s; a miss, a snap or a wind-in eases back over ease_s. The line leaves the tip where the rod is drawn this frame.

The hand's faces

The game shaded each side face of a hand prism by the direction from the prism's middle to the middle of that side. On a round finger the two are the same; on the flattened back of the hand (1.97 by 0.70 tool units) its four slanted faces were shaded as if they pointed up to about 50° away from where they do: the patchy back of the hand. hexel::prism now shades each side by its true normal, the side edge crossed with the axis turned outward (commit 8a769c43), as the approved Hex Tool Bench did. Every hand here is built by a port of that function. The fingers are unchanged.

Tuning

Each tab's Tune holds its values as sliders and prints the assets/config/held.ron they make. The stroke values replace the three swing_* values (held-swing decision 1); the rod's are its rod block. Names in the design tables: windup_s, windup_rad, strike_s, strike_rad, impact_hold_s, recoil_rad, recover_s, stroke_target_s, drop_m, camera_kick_rad; draw_rad, draw_lift_m, flick_rad, flick_s, settle_rad, nibble_rad, bite_rad, fight_rad, fight_shake_rad, crank_m, spring_hz, damping. The rod-motion table gives numbers but no names for the rest; this page names them bob_rad, nibble_s, bite_shake_rad, bite_shake_hz, fight_shake_hz, crank_hz, roll_rad, catch_rad, catch_s and ease_s. The table gives no value for fight_shake_rad, the bite's shake or the bob; 0.06, 0.04 and 0.015 rad are this page's.

Game-exact and approximated

  • Exact: the four tools' hexes, the hands' prisms, each tool's butt, rotation, scale, fist and rod tip, from assets/models/held_tools.ron; the hex mesh and its face shades (hexel::mesh, shade); the hand's shade 0.62 + 0.38·max(n·light, 0) against (0.3, 0.8, 0.55) in eye space at the rest pose; the unlit held material tinted by the light at the eye; the 60° camera; today's chop and sway; the cast's physics, the float's two spheres and its dips (0.035 m nibble, 0.14 m bite); the line's sag (0.6 m slack, 0.1 + 0.5·(1 − tension) on a fish) held 5 cm over the water; the break times.
  • Approximated: the ground, water, trees and sky are simple stand-ins in the game's palette, lit by three.js lights, not the game's terrain, water and atmosphere shaders. The light on the tool in hand is the moon plus the lantern at the hand, worked out the way the page lights the ground, standing in for the game's light field. A tool head's tier tint is not shown. The fish is scripted: when it nibbles and bites, and how it runs, are set by the page, not by the game's schools. The rod's roll is about the view's forward axis.
  • Not modelled: the rod does not bend (rod-motion decision 6): it is a rigid hex model, and the pitch and shake carry the fight. A bend can come later if wanted.
  • The screenshots for this page were rendered in a cloud container without a GPU (SwiftShader); they show the look, not the frame rate.
