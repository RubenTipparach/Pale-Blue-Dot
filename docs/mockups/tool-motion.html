Each tab has one 3D view. Dig, Chop, Punch and Fish have a Today / Proposed switch in it. Day / Night at the top applies to every tab. Nothing moves until you hold the mouse on the view or press a button; only the world moves on its own (water, a lantern's flame).

The stroke: four stages on an arc (Dig, Chop, Proposed)

  • Windup (windup_s, eased out): the arm raises the tool up and back toward the right shoulder by raise_rad, the tool tipping back windup_rad on top, and the arm gathers in by coil_m.
  • Anticipation (anticipate_s, anticipate_rad): at the top it settles a little further back and holds: a slow ease into a coiled pause, the beat before the release.
  • Hit (strike_s, eased in with t³ so it is fastest at contact, then impact_hold_s): the sweep forward and down to full reach (reach_rad, the arm stretched by extend_m), the head landing at the crosshair, and a held frame on contact with the hit marker, chips and the optional camera kick.
  • Recoil (recoil_s, recoil_rad, then recover_s): the tool kicks back up off the block with a slight overshoot while the arm follows through low (follow_rad), then eases to rest or into the next windup.
  • The fist moves on an arc about a virtual shoulder (shoulder, eye space, m), never a straight line; the arm, cuff and sleeve swing with it. The stage name shows live under the view; Slow ×0.25 slows it to see each stage, and Show poses freezes on the four keyed poses (rest, top, hit, recoil) in turn.
  • The strokes keep one steady beat from block to block: a block of break time T and the 0.15 s before the next one (between_s, dig.ron) get N = max(1, round((T + 0.15) / stroke_target_s)) strokes of one period P = (T + 0.15) / N, the last on the break. If a stroke is longer than P, its windup, anticipation and recover shrink in proportion; the hit never does.
  • Timing by weight: the windup, anticipation and impact hold are scaled by the tool's weight (pickaxe_weight, axe_weight, shovel_weight) and by the tier, 1 + tier_slow × (3 − tier), so stone is a little slower than steel. exaggerate scales the raise, the recoil and the camera kick together.
  • The break times are the game's, assets/config/dig.ron: the stone tier's matrix (pickaxe on stone 1.2 s, rock 1.6 s, ore 2.0 s; shovel on dirt 0.5 s; axe on wood 0.6 s) divided by the tier's speed (1, 1.6, 2.4, 3.6).
  • Today is the game's chop (held::hold): a sine at 9 rad/s, chop = sin(phase).max(-0.2), pitching the tool 0.85 rad forward about a fixed fist and dropping it 6 cm: a wrist flick, slowest at the moment it should land. It has no hits of its own, and letting go snaps the tool to rest.
  • At the hit the arm also swings in toward the middle of the view (sweep_rad, about the shoulder's upright), so the head lands at the crosshair rather than off to the right where the fist rests.
  • The shovel (the owner: "shovel tips down and should move forward like a sliding motion into the dirt, tip and pull up dirt", with a GIF of a man digging a bank) has no overhead arc. The windup tips the spade down, entry_deg below the horizontal with the tip leading, and brings the fist low and back. The hit slides it forward along the ground into the dirt by slide_m, sinking slide_drop_m: a push, not a stab. The fist stays low: at the hit it is fist_drop_m below and fist_fwd_m ahead of where it rests, across_m in toward the middle. The recoil tips the spade about the point where the blade is in the dirt (lever_rad), then pulls the dirt up (lift_m) and back (pull_m) with a clump on the blade, which tips off as it returns. The arm follows most of the spade's turn (arm_follow 0.7) and the fist stays on its own side (across_m 0), so the wrist never bends back hard, and the fist drops low (fist_drop_m 0.16) so the shoulder stays down (the owner: "the shoulder is too high"); at the low tip-down the hand goes off the bottom of the view, as it would digging low (the owner: "looks like it hurts the angle", then "the camera just cant see the hand, which is fine").
  • In Proposed the hand is the proposed hand (below), and its sleeve is sleeve_extra tool units longer at its far end, so the arm still runs off the screen at full reach.

The fist matches the owner’s two photos of their own: it punches forward into the view, the four fingers curled tight into the palm and seen as a column down the leading edge, the back of the hand toward the right and the eye, the thumb on top at the front, the fist about as long as it is tall; the forearm comes in from the lower right, aimed from a virtual elbow (elbow_x, elbow_y, elbow_z) that follows the fist by elbow_follow, so the sleeve stays in view through the whole stroke; the wrist is straight, slimmed to the hand and one tone with the forearm, so the heel of the hand runs into it with no step (bend_rad, guard_cock_rad and hit_cock_rad are 0; they can still turn the hand on the wrist, the forearm keeping its line). The guard rests fist_raise_m up and fist_in_m in from a tool’s grip, turned guard_twist_rad, so it is in view between punches. hands_weight scales the windup, the anticipation and the hold: a bare hand is lighter and quicker than a tool.

The twelve principles, here

  • Squash and stretch: the tools are rigid, so the arm gathers at the top (coil_m) and stretches at the hit (extend_m).
  • Anticipation: the coiled pause before the hit; the rod draws back before the flick, and dips before it lifts on a catch (catch_dip_rad).
  • Staging: the tool head lands at the crosshair and stays in frame against the block at the hit.
  • Pose to pose: four keyed poses (rest, top, hit, recoil) blended by the stage curves; Show poses shows them.
  • Follow-through and overlapping action: the head lags the fist on the windup by lag_s and carries overlap_rad past it at the hit, settling in the recoil; the arm follows through low; the rod tip wobbles after the flick (flick_wobble_rad) and the line follows the tip.
  • Slow in and slow out: every held pose is eased into and out of; the hit alone arrives at full speed.
  • Arcs: the fist swings about the shoulder, and the rod tip arcs over on the cast.
  • Secondary action: chips, the hit marker, the camera kick (0 unless raised), the sleeve and cuff, the idle breathing sway between strokes.
  • Timing: one steady beat from the dig's own clock; heavier tools wind up slower and hold the impact longer; lower tiers a little slower.
  • Exaggeration: one exaggerate value (0–1.5) pushes or calms the raise, recoil and kick together.
  • Solid drawing: the Hand tab shows each pose from any side; the fist must read as one solid fist and the arm must leave the screen edge.
  • Appeal: the owner's call, on this page.

Fish: how the fight works

The line is the game's own rules, pbd_core::fishing, with fauna.ron's numbers and the species' pull and strength. Left button: hold to charge and let go to cast, click when the float goes under to strike, then hold to reel. Right click winds an empty line in.

  • Tension (the meter) starts at 0.2 on the strike. While you reel it rises 0.35 a second, plus 0.8 × the fish's pull × its stamina while it runs. With the button up it falls 0.7 a second.
  • The fish runs and rests in turns: a run of 0.8–2 s at its full pull, a rest of 1.2–3 s. While it runs, reeling takes in less line (1.8 m/s × (1 − 0.6 × min(pull / 1.5, 1))) and the tension climbs much faster; with the button up it takes line back out at 1.2 × pull × stamina m/s.
  • Stamina falls 0.08 a second while the fish runs against a line over 0.25 tension, never under 0.15: a tired fish pulls less.
  • The line snaps when the tension reaches 1. The fish is landed when the line is down to 1.3 m, or the float is in water under 0.15 m deep.
  • So the fight is: reel while it rests, ease off when it runs (the tension shoots up and the line goes out), and reel again. Holding the button the whole way snaps the line in about two seconds; never reeling never lands it.
  • The meter is green to 0.55, amber to 0.8 and red above, as the game's HUD. The prompts are the game's (fish.rs): "Hold to reel - ease off when it runs", "The line snapped - the … is gone", "<name> - N cm", and the others.
  • What shows the run: in the game, only the line: the float stays pulled under at the bite's depth through the fight, and while the fish runs the place the line enters the water weaves 0.12 × pull rad either side. The page adds ripples there while it runs, and its readout under the view names the run and the stamina. The game has no run prompt; a cue could be added if this is still hard to read.
  • Land and Snap hook a fish and let a bot play it: Land reels while the tension is low and eases off when it climbs or the fish runs; Snap holds the button down. Fight hooks one and hands it to you. Play all casts, waits for the bite, strikes and lands it.
  • Approximated: when a fish comes to the float (the game's schools) is the page's: 0.8–2.4 s after the splash time. The nibbles, bite window, fight, snap and landing are the game's rules.

The rod (Proposed)

rod-motion design 2: a damped spring toward each phase's target (spring_hz, damping), so a phase change never pops. The cast is an overhead cast in four stages (owner, 2026-11-17: "the casting fishing line the hand should be raised above shoulder"): the windup raises the fist on an arc about the shoulder, cast_raise_m up and cast_back_m back toward the head, so it ends above the right shoulder with the rod pointing up and back, draw_rad back from rest; the anticipation is a held coil at the top while the charge fills; the hit, on release, drives the arm forward and down along the arc to chest height (cast_reach_m past rest) as the rod flicks to −flick_rad in flick_s, pointing at the water; the recoil is the tip's wobble and the settle; a nibble twitches the tip; a bite dips it; while a fish is on the rod is held up at fight_rad, pulled down by run_dip_rad while the fish runs, rolled toward its pull up to roll_rad, and shakes by fight_shake_rad × the live tension; reeling turns the fist in a crank_m circle; a catch dips then lifts by catch_rad. Today: the rod stands still in every phase.

The hand (the Hand tab)

Tuning

Each tab's Tune holds its values as sliders and prints the assets/config/held.ron they make: the stroke block (held-swing) and the rod block (rod-motion). The design tables name the first values; the page names the rest (raise_rad, coil_m, anticipate_s, anticipate_rad, reach_rad, extend_m, sweep_rad, recoil_s, follow_rad, lag_s, overlap_rad, exaggerate, shoulder, the weights, tier_slow, sleeve_extra; the shovel block; and three for the rod beyond the game's block, run_dip_rad, flick_wobble_rad and catch_dip_rad). The rod block prints exactly the game's fields (held.rs, merged), each a positive size, rate or time that the code gives its sign, so it pastes into held.ron; the page's three extra rod values and the hand's shade are printed as comments after it. The stroke and shovel blocks are proposed (held-swing) and replace the swing_* values once the game reads them.

Game-exact and approximated

  • Exact: the four tools' hexes, the hands' prisms, each tool's butt, rotation, scale, fist and rod tip, from assets/models/held_tools.ron; the hex mesh and its face shades (hexel::mesh, shade); the hand's shade (hexel::prism); the unlit held material tinted by the light at the eye; the 60° camera; today's chop and sway; the line's rules and numbers; the float's dips and the line's sag (0.6 m slack, 0.1 + 0.5·(1 − tension) on a fish) held 5 cm over the water; the HUD's prompts and meter colours; the break times.
  • Approximated: the ground, water, trees and sky are simple stand-ins in the game's palette, lit by three.js lights, not the game's shaders, and there is no exposure or tone curve except the Hand tab's Game tone switch. The light on the tool in hand is the moon plus the lantern near the hand, standing in for the game's light field at the eye. A tool head's tier tint is not shown. When a fish comes to the float is the page's. The bank is a step behind the water's edge, so a fish landed at 1.3 m of line is drawn at the edge.
  • Not modelled: the rod does not bend (rod-motion decision 6).
