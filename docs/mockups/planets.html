The Halcyon System

All Pale Blue Dot mockups (the tracker)

Twelve bodies round one star: three worlds you can live on, two gas giants you skim for fuel, and seven moons and rocks that each play differently: an airless moon in the home sky, a volcanic one, an ice shell, a hazy methane moon, a hollow one full of caves, an ocean moon that always faces its giant, and a 300 m asteroid. Every body with ground is made of the same 2.833 m hex cells, because its radius sits on the ladder R = 300 m × 2L−7.

ProposalNone of this is built. The game today has one planet (the 4,800 m home world, generator TENEBRIS v5) and an 850 m placeholder moon sphere. Names, sizes, gravities and rosters below are recommendations for the owner to accept or change; the decisions are listed at the end.
Orbits are analytic and on rails: each body's position is a function of world time alone. Not to scale: sizes are enlarged and moon orbits spread so you can see them. True numbers are on the cards. The night side shows each world's own lights.

Every body

The deck

Click a card to inspect it above. Chips say whether a body has air (and so sky, weather and clouds) and life. An airless body has neither, by the world rules in CLAUDE.md.

Hex size is fixed

Every body sits on the ladder

Tile width is 1.2087 × R / 2L, measured on the unit sphere. Holding it at the Tenebris gold standard of 2.833 m ties the radius to the subdivision level, so a radius is chosen from this ladder and never authored first. A cell dug on Lantern is the same cell dug at home.

The two giants have no cells: you cannot stand on them. Their cloud-top radii are still taken from the ladder's next rungs (L12 and L13), so the system has one size scale throughout. Cell counts are 10 × 4L + 2, twelve of them pentagons on every body.

Mockups are lit at night

How the vignettes are lit

Each vignette has a time of day. At night every lamp, window, fire, lava pool and glowing crystal lights what is near it, wherever the camera is: the light is baked into the faces when the vignette is built, the way the engine's block light will carry it, with the same falloff towns.html uses. Fires, lava and crystals burn all day; lamps and windows come on at dusk. The moon (or the giant's shine on a moon) is kept dim enough that the lamps read. Airless bodies have a black sky by day, stars and all, and no haze.

From orbit too

Lights on the night side

The globes carry a pixel-art lights map on their dark side: town lights on the three habitable worlds and Tidewater, lava on Cinder, violet cave glow through Hollow's sinkholes, one outpost on Murk. It fades in across the terminator, so dusk on a world reads from space.

Where the art comes from

Pixel art, in the solitaire collection's style

The game's own art, copied byte-identical into planets/game/ (its README names each source): the vignettes' hex columns wear the terrain atlas (assets/tilesets/atlas.png), each cell its biome sheet's ground on top, the ground-into-earth tile on the first metre of wall, earth then stone below, as planet_surface.wgsl picks them; trees are the game's hex-prism trees in the sheet's wood and leaf tiles; houses, habs, posts and decks wear the settlement materials (assets/textures/settlement); the landed ship is the Kestrel and the boat the Tern (assets/models/vehicles, loaded with GLTFLoader); the hotbar shows the game's item icons and atlas block tiles. All of it is sampled nearest without mipmaps. Bodies the game does not have yet borrow the nearest sheet: Cinder is the basalt sheet with its own sulfur and lava tiles, Hollow is sporewood, Murk the desert sheet tinted.

Mockup-only art: the surface maps, night-light maps, portraits and resource icons are pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit, the solitaire collection's style (.claude/skills/balatro-pixel-art): a tiny native canvas, one bold subject, three to five tones a material lit from the top left, a dark seam between parts, a 1 px outline, single-pixel glints, no anti-aliasing, and every colour from the Sweaft-64 palette (the kit refuses to save a pixel off it). Maps are 128 x 64, drawn per pixel from noise on the sphere so the globes have no seam, with Bayer dithering for soft edges. Portraits are 48 x 48 (each body's own map wrapped on a lit disc, its night lights on the dark side), icons 32 x 32, all shown at whole-number scales. The script is tools/art/planets_art.py; it took three passes against contact sheets beside six of the solitaire collection's sprites (fixes: thinner cloud decks, solid polar ice on Lantern, one crack network on Rime, larger ice crystals, a lit shoulder that keeps each colour's hue).
