About this mockup: the system, and what is proposed

Eighteen bodies round one star. The home world is Tenebris, the planet the game already builds, and its sister planet Sequoia rides the next orbit out; Tenebris's other bodies (barren Crag, icy Frostbite, their moons and its belt rocks) are here too, beside the system's own worlds: four worlds you can live on, two gas giants you cannot land on (an airship works in their cloud decks, over a sea of hydrogen), and moons and rocks that each play differently. Two neighbouring stars, Tallow and Gemel, are on the star map, reached by proposed star lanes. Every body with ground is made of the same 2.833 m hex cells, because its radius sits on the ladder R = 300 m × 2L−7.

ProposalNone of this is built. The game today has one planet (the 4,800 m home world, generator TENEBRIS v5) and an 850 m placeholder moon sphere. Names, sizes, gravities and rosters below are recommendations for the owner to accept or change; the decisions are listed at the end.
Orbits are analytic and on rails: each body's position is a function of world time alone. Not to scale: sizes are enlarged and moon orbits spread so you can see them. True numbers are on the cards. The night side shows each world's own lights.

How you get there

Star lanes: the travel page's lanes, between stars

Nothing here is a warp drive. Ships keep the Newtonian flight, limits and dampeners of the travel page (travel.html), and the fusion and antimatter tiers of ship-tiers.html never fly faster than their thrust allows. What crosses light years is a lane, the same idea as the travel page's lane gates, which already carry a ship from one gate to its partner "with nothing simulated in between". A star lane is a pair of such gates, one at the edge of each system, 1,800 km from its star (past Glaucus at Aubade). Fly in aligned and slow; the passage takes 30 s of play per light year; the ship comes out of the far gate in that star's frame at the speed it went in. The game's systems are already drawn small (Tenebris orbits 300 km from Aubade), so the light years are the map's distances, not a speed any ship reaches.

The first passage to a star carries a gate kit one way, uncrewed: a seed run. The kit builds the far gate, and only then is the lane open both ways, so nobody is ever stranded. Gates are a late goal: they take the antimatter collider's power (the third ship tier) and platinum, which is why Lode matters. World time passes in a passage as in any other play.

A neighbour, 6.3 ly · new

The Tallow System

A red dwarf, 3% of Aubade's light, so its living zone sits only 45 to 70 km out and its worlds there turn one face to it. It flares. Why go: Wick, the one world with a twilight ring of black-leaved life between a scorched dayside and a night of ice; emberglass, dust fused by flares on the iron world Sconce; and Censer, a dark red giant to skim. Airless Sconce and Snuff have no sky, weather or life.

A neighbour, 8.1 ly · new

The Gemel System

An orange dwarf, Gemel A, with a white-dwarf companion, Gemel B, riding far out: two suns in every sky, one of them a hard white point. Why go: Fallow, a cold golden steppe with lakes and glaciers and nobody on it, the open land for the player's own towns and herds; platinum on the metal rock Lode; and slate from Shale. Shale and Lode are airless and lifeless.

Day 0
Tenebris's own bodies, in the home system · mockup only, no game code

Tenebris and Sequoia

The home world is Tenebris (the owner, 2026-11-14: "pale blue = tenebris, so call it that, sequoia is the sister planet"): the game's planet, its generator TENEBRIS v5, its radius, orbit and look, unchanged, with no save touched. Its sister Sequoia rides the next orbit out, 380 km from Aubade, as it rode beyond Tenebris in Tenebris's own system. Tenebris's other bodies joined them in Aubade: Crag inside Ochre, Frostbite beyond Glaucus, Selene as the home world's second moon, Sprout round Sequoia, Quartz round Crag, Hoar (Tenebris's Rime, renamed) round Frostbite, and the ten belt rocks in Aubade's belt.

Onto the ladder

In Tenebris only the main planet held the 2.833 m cell: its other bodies took whatever level subdivisions_for_radius gave (held at 7), so their cells ran from 0.68 m to 3.40 m. Here every body has the same cell. The scale is set by Tenebris itself: its 300 m is the home world's 4,800 m, sixteen times. The rule: sixteen times the Tenebris radius, down to the rung at or below it, never under 300 m. It needs no exception: Sequoia (360 m, 5,760 m at sixteen times) lands on 4,800 m (L11), the same rung as Tenebris, and Luna (80 m) lands on 1,200 m, Lantern's rung, which is why Lantern stands in for it. Gravity is Tenebris's own, so Sequoia is still the heavier sister (1.1 g).

Tenebris's textures, as copied

Byte-identical copies of tenebris-rs/assets/textures at commit ef98651, in planets/tenebris/ (its README names each). None was a Git LFS stub, so none is a stand-in. Sequoia and the barren and icy bodies wear them nearest-sampled, 16 px a tile, with Tenebris's own runtime tints (Crag's rust, Frostbite's glacier, the classic leaves). The home world wears the game's own atlas, as it does today. The animals are boxes wearing Tenebris's animal atlas (animals.png, one 32 px column per species); the four planned Sequoia species and the new systems' animals wear mockup-only columns drawn in the same layout.

No landing on a gas giant · proposed, not built

Into Amber

The owner, 2026-11-14: "there is no landing on gas giants, some kind of air ship could deploy in the atmosphere though, but at a certain point in the atmosphere should just be an ocean of hydrogen". A giant has no ground and no cells. A ship docks at the floating skimmer platform or drops an airship: an aerostat whose envelope holds hot hydrogen, so it floats in a band of the cloud decks and works there, scooping helium-3 and hydrogen (the fusion tier's fuel on industry.html). Below the band the pressure, heat and wind climb to the limit, and the owner again, the same day: "gas giants will generally destroy any spacecraft that reaches a certain depth, well before they get to the ocean". Past the limit every craft is destroyed, airship or ship, of every tier; push the airship there and it is lost. Far below, past a long zone where nothing survives, lies the sea of liquid hydrogen, metallic hydrogen under it: it is seen, never reached. Depths are in the game's metres, scaled to these small worlds; the view is not to scale.

Every body

The deck

Click a card to open it in Selected body. Chips say whether a body has air (and so sky, weather and clouds) and life. An airless body has neither, by the world rules in CLAUDE.md.

Hex size is fixed

Every body sits on the ladder

Tile width is 1.2087 × R / 2L, measured on the unit sphere. Holding it at the Tenebris gold standard of 2.833 m ties the radius to the subdivision level, so a radius is chosen from this ladder and never authored first. A cell dug on Lantern is the same cell dug at home.

The two giants have no cells: you cannot stand on them. Their cloud-top radii are still taken from the ladder's next rungs (L12 and L13), so the system has one size scale throughout. Cell counts are 10 × 4L + 2, twelve of them pentagons on every body.

Mockups are lit at night

How the vignettes are lit

Each vignette has a time of day. At night every lamp, window, fire, lava pool and glowing crystal lights what is near it, wherever the camera is: the light is baked into the faces when the vignette is built, the way the engine's block light will carry it, with the same falloff towns.html uses. Fires, lava and crystals burn all day; lamps and windows come on at dusk. The moon (or the giant's shine on a moon) is kept dim enough that the lamps read. Airless bodies have a black sky by day, stars and all, and no haze.

From orbit too

Lights on the night side

The globes carry a pixel-art lights map on their dark side: town lights on the three habitable worlds and Tidewater, lava on Cinder, violet cave glow through Hollow's sinkholes, one outpost on Murk. It fades in across the terminator, so dusk on a world reads from space.

Where the art comes from

Pixel art, in the solitaire collection's style

The game's own art, copied byte-identical into planets/game/ (its README names each source): the vignettes' hex columns wear the terrain atlas (assets/tilesets/atlas.png), each cell its biome sheet's ground on top, the ground-into-earth tile on the first metre of wall, earth then stone below, as planet_surface.wgsl picks them; trees are the game's hex-prism trees in the sheet's wood and leaf tiles; houses, habs, posts and decks wear the settlement materials (assets/textures/settlement); the landed ship is the Kestrel and the boat the Tern (assets/models/vehicles, loaded with GLTFLoader); the hotbar shows the game's item icons and atlas block tiles. All of it is sampled nearest without mipmaps. Bodies the game does not have yet borrow the nearest sheet: Cinder is the basalt sheet with its own sulfur and lava tiles, Hollow is sporewood, Murk the desert sheet tinted.

Mockup-only art: the surface maps, night-light maps, portraits and resource icons are pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit, the solitaire collection's style (.claude/skills/balatro-pixel-art): a tiny native canvas, one bold subject, three to five tones a material lit from the top left, a dark seam between parts, a 1 px outline, single-pixel glints, no anti-aliasing, and every colour from the Sweaft-64 palette (the kit refuses to save a pixel off it). Maps are 128 x 64, drawn per pixel from noise on the sphere so the globes have no seam, with Bayer dithering for soft edges. Portraits are 48 x 48 (each body's own map wrapped on a lit disc, its night lights on the dark side), icons 32 x 32, all shown at whole-number scales. The script is tools/art/planets_art.py; it took three passes against contact sheets beside six of the solitaire collection's sprites (fixes: thinner cloud decks, solid polar ice on Lantern, one crack network on Rime, larger ice crystals, a lit shoulder that keeps each colour's hue).

The gas giants' airship and cloud decks are drawn in the page: the envelope wears the game's cloth, the gondola its iron and plaster, the decks a noise of each giant's own map colours.
