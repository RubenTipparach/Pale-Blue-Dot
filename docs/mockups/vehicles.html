Weather at the craft

What is modelled
  • All craft are rigid bodies stepped at 240 Hz (60 Hz x 4 substeps, as the engine's Avian runs), on a sphere of the engine's sea radius, 4,799.5 m. Gravity points at the centre.
  • Hulls are cells. Buoyancy is summed over the voxels of the hull envelope against the same sea function the water mesh draws, so heave, pitch and roll come from the waves themselves.
  • Wind has a log-law height profile and gusts that ride along with the wind. Rain adds a downdraft and stronger gusts, wets the Kestrel's wing (lower CLmax, more drag), and fills open hulls.
  • Kestrel: each wing panel, the tail and the fin is a foil with stall. The rotors tilt from 90 to 0 deg, lose thrust with inflow, gain it in ground effect, and drag sideways in a crosswind. Assist is attitude hold and vertical-speed hold in the hover.
  • Tern: the sail is a foil whose boom swings to the sheet limit. The keel and rudder are hydrofoils. Resistance rises sharply at hull speed, sqrt(gL/2pi), which is 1.6x Earth's at 25 m/s².
  • Loon: each stroke is blade drag against the water. A stroke on one side yaws the canoe. Waves over the gunwale and rain fill it, and the water sloshes to the low side.
  • Leaving a craft changes who commands it, never whether it exists. It keeps being simulated. A boat you leave drifts or rides its mooring. A Kestrel left in flight converts to the hover and lets itself down.
