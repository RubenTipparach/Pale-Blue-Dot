Stairs and collision

What is modelled
  • The engine's cell. Every hex is 2.833 m flat to flat and every layer 1.000 m, the gold standard in CLAUDE.md. Walls, floors, stairs and doors are cut to it; nothing is scaled to fit.
  • Walls sit on edges, floors in cells. A wall is a 0.3 m (timber) or 0.5 m (stone) slab on a cell's shared edge, so a one-cell room is 2.5 m across inside, not a solid block. The town wall and keep are whole masonry cells.
  • A storey is three layers, 3 m floor to floor, 2.8 m clear under the boards.
  • Three stairs, all landing on layer lines. A straight flight crosses two cells and climbs a storey in 16 risers of 0.1875 m on 0.354 m treads (28°). A newel stair turns once per storey in one cell: 15 winders of 0.2 m, and 120° per metre, so a door at any layer is always in the middle of an edge. Street steps climb one terrace in one cell: 6 risers of 0.167 m.
  • The walker is walking.rs: a 0.3 m radius body 1.8 m tall, eye at 1.6 m, 8 m/s (14 sprinting), a 12 m/s jump under 25 m/s², a 1.05 m step for terraces, a floor from the highest of several footprint points and a ceiling from the lowest.
  • Proposed: a stair's walking surface is its pitch line, a grounded walker is held to a floor up to 0.35 m below, and thin geometry (walls, jambs, furniture, posts, people) pushes the body out along its face so it slides instead of stopping.
  • A newel stair ends in a 30° landing and a rail, so a walker who keeps turning meets the rail, not the well. A wider landing would take headroom from the turn below.
  • Light. Sky light is dimmed on faces under a roof, standing in for the baked skylight of the voxel-light change. Hearths and the forge are the only lights indoors.
