Proposal, not built

Nothing on this page is in the engine. It is a design for stations and outposts, with a mockup scene to argue it. The station portraits are pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit, the solitaire collection's style (tools/art/stations_art.py): 96 x 64 sprites shown at 2x, nearest neighbour.

What you can dock at

Six kinds of station

A station is a place on rails: it rides an analytic orbit like a moon does, so it is always where the clock says it is, and its market, jobs and people stay put between visits. Each kind has a different job in the economy, a different law, and a different reason to come back.

Arriving

The docking flow

  1. Hail at 20 km. The station answers with who it is, who runs it and what it wants from you: a pad number, a toll, or a scan. A smuggler den answers only to a known transponder or a password from a contact.
  2. Clearance and a berth. Hub berths sit on the spin axis and never move; ring berths and bays ride the spin. The berth is reserved for 5 minutes of game time; the approach corridor lights up for you (the green lane in the scene).
  3. Enter the control volume (2 km). From here the ship's pose and velocity are written in the station's local frame. Assisted flight holds you to the corridor's speed cone and, for a ring berth, matches the spin before the last 50 m.
  4. Customs, where there is any. Military posts scan every hold. Trade hubs scan one in five. Mining outposts do not care. Contraband found is fined or seized, and your standing with the station's owner drops.
  5. Final approach and clamp. Under 50 m the assist takes the last metres at under 1 m/s; magnetic clamps take the ship. Docking fees are paid on clamp, by size class.
  6. Step off. The ship's occupancy changes; it does not stop existing. Its ID, pose in the berth, hold, damage and owner persist, and it is boardable from the hangar. You walk off as a passenger, simulated in the station's frame.

The cone is a limit the assist holds, not a rail. A pilot with assist off can fly it by hand; touching a gate over its limit costs a fine, and hitting the hull costs damage.

Inside the Meridian ring

The concourse

A ring is a street bent into a circle. "Down" is outward, the floor curves up ahead and behind you, and every shop on the station is a walk spinward or antispinward from the lift. Hover or tap a room.

Pick a room

Each room is a place the player walks to; the ones that open a screen say which.

Where a passenger lives

Frames, from the system to your boots

CLAUDE.md already requires it: station passengers simulate in the station's local frame. A ring adds one more frame, the spinning one, so walking on the concourse is ordinary walking on a floor with gravity pointing outward. The chain is:

Systemf64, orbital time. The station's rails (an analytic orbit) give its pose at any time.
Stationf32, origin at the hub. Ships in the control volume, the hub, berths on the axis.
Ringf32, spinning at a fixed rate about the hub axis. The concourse, ring berths, passengers.
PassengerWalks with the ring's own gravity (centripetal, 9.8 m/s² at 60 m). Rebased with the ring at a tick boundary.

Spin numbers in the scene are real: a 60 m ring at 3.86 rpm gives 1.0 g at the floor and about 0.85 g a deck in. Coriolis is small enough at that size to ignore for walking; we propose leaving it out, and keeping thrown objects and spilled cargo inside the ring frame too.

For the owner

Decisions / questions for the owner

1. Do stations spin?

Options: (a) rings spin and give gravity, hubs do not; (b) no spin, artificial gravity everywhere; (c) only some kinds spin.

Recommendation: (a). It is what makes a station feel like a station, it costs one extra frame we need anyway, and outposts on rocks can use the rock's own weak gravity or magnetic boots.

2. Are stations built from cells like the planets?

Options: (a) authored meshes, never editable; (b) built from hex cells, so the player can mine or build onto one; (c) authored shell, cell interiors.

Recommendation: (c). An authored hull reads well from outside; a cell interior lets the player rent and furnish a room, and keeps one building system.

3. Can the player own a station?

Options: no; a module on someone else's station; a whole outpost later.

Recommendation: a rented room and hangar first, a mining outpost of their own later. Either way it is a stored record on the durable path, like a town.

4. How hard is the docking?

Options: fully automatic once cleared; assisted inside the corridor (proposed); fully manual.

Recommendation: assisted inside the corridor, with an "auto-dock" upgrade sold at shipyards. It keeps the Newtonian flight honest and the last 50 m tense without being a chore.

5. Are stations lit inside the planet's shadow?

The scene shows it: in eclipse, everything you see is the station's own lamps. Recommendation: yes, every station kind has floodlights on its hull, warm windows and lit approach lanes; the lights are block lights in the same system the towns use, so they light the hull wherever the camera is.

Mockup for Pale Blue Dot. Scene: three.js r128, stations built of boxes, hex prisms and a ring of straight segments, every surface a pixel texture sampled nearest; each lamp lights the hull per pixel and moves with what carries it (so a lamp lights what is near it wherever the camera is), sun shadows from a shadow map, the planet's shadow computed from the orbit.
