Herds, Hunters and Glowmoths

Animals for the home planet, biome by biome: who grazes, who hunts, who sleeps by day, what glows after dark, what can be tamed and what each one drops. Fish already swim (world/fauna); this is everything that walks, flies and crawls. Nothing on this page exists in the game yet.

Who lives where

Each biome of generator version 6 (bigger-biomes: fields, desert, jungle, swamp, mountains, tundra, beach, ocean) gets a short roster: one or two grazers, a hunter, a bird and something small. The ocean's roster is the fish list that already ships. Portraits are drawn at the size the field guide would show them.

How they behave

Herd, flee, hunt

Land animals reuse the schools' approach (fauna): packed arrays stepped on the CPU at a fixed rate, deterministic from a seed, one draw per kind. A herd shares a goal and keeps together; a grazer that sees a hunter inside its fear radius bolts away from it and the rest of the herd follows. A hunter picks the nearest prey in reach, chases at its run speed and gives up after a few seconds. In this mockup the hunter always misses; in the game a catch removes the prey from the herd and leaves its drops.

Day, dusk and night

Every species has an activity window. Grazers lie down at night in a tight group; foxes, dust hounds, stripe cats and pale wolves wake at dusk. Their eyes catch your lamplight. Fireflies, glowmoths and bog toads glow: fireflies drift over fields and swamp, glowmoths gather round the nearest lamp, toads' spots pulse by the water. A glow is light: each swarm lights what is near it, like a small lamp.

Seasons and migration

A year is 100 days (orbit-and-seasons), and day 0 is the northern summer. Shagback herds leave the tundra for the winter quarter and come back in spring. Larks leave the fields in winter. Snow hares and pale wolves turn white for winter and brown-grey in summer. The tropics have no winter to speak of, so the jungle roster is the same all year. Pick a season above to see it.

Taming and husbandry

Tame by feeding. Hold a species' food (grain for woolbacks and hens, turnips for plodders, kale for ridge goats, dates for sand striders) and walk up crouched. Each feed fills a trust meter; full trust and the animal follows you. Lead it into a pen and it is livestock.

Livestock is yours and is saved. A tamed animal is a stored record on the durable path (world-persistence), with its own ID, its species, its seed (so it keeps its size and coat) and its owner. Wild animals are derived from the seed and the clock and are never saved, the way schools are not.

Pens give produce on a timer in world time, which passes only while the world is played: a woolback grows a fleece every 4 days and is shorn with shears; a hen lays an egg a day; a plodder gives milk each day with a pail. Fed livestock breeds when two share a pen: a new animal whose seed mixes its parents', so its size and coat sit between theirs.

Mounts: sand striders carry a pack (8 slots) and walk you across the desert at 6 m/s. They are the only mount on this page; a ship is still the way to travel far.

What they drop

Drops are items for the inventory grid. Hunting is a choice, never a requirement: every drop a wild animal gives, a tamed one gives without being killed (wool, milk, eggs, feathers, glow), except meat and hide.

Rosters are per body

The rule the fish already follow holds for every animal: a body's roster is explicit data, no species lives on two bodies, and an airless body's roster is empty. Barren, frozen and asteroid catalogue worlds are lifeless; pick "Barren moon" above and the patch is empty apart from your lamps. A future living world gets its own animals, never a copy of these.

Rosters are append-only, like the fish list, because a tamed animal is saved as its species' index. Adding or retuning wild animals never touches a saved world's terrain; it only changes what roams it.

About the art

The portraits and drop icons are painted by code, not by hand: tools/art/animals_art.py drives the hand-drawn-illustration kit, which copies the owner's friend's marker method (flat block, hatching in the shape's own tones, form crescents, a same-hue edge). It gets close; it is not the friend's work. Every four-legged portrait comes from one function fed the same body-plan numbers as the 3D creatures above. The 3D creatures are primitives (ellipsoids, cylinders, cones) as a stand-in for real models.

Decisions / questions for the owner

  1. Do animals ever attack the player? Recommendation: no, not in the first version. Hunters chase prey only and flee from the player at close range. Danger can come later, behind a world setting.
  2. Is hunting in, or only husbandry? Recommendation: both, with every non-meat drop available from tamed animals, so a player who never hunts misses nothing but meat and hide.
  3. How many animals on screen? Recommendation: at most 40 land animals within 150 m, despawned behind the player, in packed arrays like schools. This needs a perf_suite run on real hardware before it lands.
  4. Do predators thin the herds for real? Recommendation: yes, but only near the player. Out of sight the population is a number per region that drifts back to its target, never a simulation.
  5. Glowing creatures as light sources? Recommendation: yes, as block light at the swarm's cell, the way lamps are (lamps-and-lanterns), with a glow jar item that is a dim portable lamp.
  6. Art: primitives, Blender models, or painted billboards? Recommendation: low-poly Blender models with pixel-art textures, the vehicle pipeline, with these portraits as the field guide's art.
