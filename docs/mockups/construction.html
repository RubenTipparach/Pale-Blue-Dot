Past the fabricator, a yard for what is too big to carry, built three ways side by side. The old way: a half-timbered house of the towns' own kit rising inside a timber scaffold, its beams lifted by a treadwheel crane that someone walks. The new way: a three-storey house set in precast panels under a steel tower crane driven from its cab. Printed: a hex printer laying a small home one 1 m layer a pass, and a parts printer printing a wing panel and a propeller for the assembly bay, which turns parts into the large items that planes, boats and buildings are made of.

About the art. The game's own (byte-identical copies in construction/game/, see its README): the ground is the terrain atlas's fields sheet (assets/tilesets/atlas.png) on 2.833 m hex columns with 1 m layers, the plots topped with its crumbly dirt tile; the old house's stone storey, half-timbered panels, timber frame, plank floors and the scaffold's timber, plank and rope, the thatch, the apron, the bay's brick and slate, the office's boards and the masts are the settlement textures stone, halftimber, timber, plank, rope, thatch, flag, rubble, brick, slate, boards and iron at their repeat_m; the hand, the brazier and the lantern post in the hotbar are the game's icons. Mockup-only: the machine and item icons and the printstone, panel, concrete, glass, polymer, crane paint, lattice, crate, container, counterweight, hazard and plate tiles are pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit (tools/art/construction_art.py), 32 x 32 and sampled nearest; the recipe item icons are the crafting mockup's; the worker in the treadwheel is the towns mockup's person, flat-coloured as it is there. Every house here is the town's cut: walls on cell edges, floors in cells, three layers a storey.

1The yard: three ways to build

West to east: old, printed, new, with the assembly bay and the parts bed behind the printer. Drag to look around, scroll or pinch to zoom; nothing moves until you move it. The print slider runs the small home from bare plot to roof in four passes, and the parts bed with it; Play runs both. The crane controls switch between the treadwheel and the tower crane: each has its own load chart and refuses a load past it, with the numbers. Walk the wheel turns the treadwheel and winds its rope. At night the brazier and the lanterns light the old site, floodlights and work lamps the new one, the print heads glow, and the ground between the pools stays dark.

2The assembly bay

A large fabricator in a shed with its own bridge crane. Its recipes are big: each makes one item with a mass and a footprint in cells, delivered as a crate on the bay floor for a crane, a cart or a vehicle to take. Printed parts come in from the parts bed outside its door. It draws power from the grid (Pits, Plants and Power) and its other inputs are the fabricator's (Forge and Fabricator). Queue a few; finished ones can be hooked on the tower crane above. Time runs a minute a second.

The bay's queue

One item at a time, in order. A crate stands where it was made until something moves it; it is saved where it stands.

3The printer's pass plan

One pass is one 1 m layer of the small home, seen from above: three cells, one storey, a roof. The head traces every wall line of the layer, eight beads of 12.5 cm on each, and travels between lines without printing (dashed). Walls are 0.3 m thick on the cell edges the blueprint names, with gaps where the door and the windows go; the floor and the roof fill the cells.

A built course is a world edit

Every piece the player sets from the scaffold, every panel the tower crane lands and every layer the printer finishes enters the durable path as an edit, naming its author: the player, or the printer by its ID on its owner's behalf. A change the world makes never overwrites the player's.

A stopped job keeps what it built

Out of printstone, out of power, or the game closed: the printed layers that finished are real and saved, and the one under way is laid again from its start. A half-raised house keeps every piece already set, and its scaffold stands until it is taken down.

Crates and scaffolds are records

A large item has a stable ID, a mass and a footprint in cells, and is saved where it stands; set down, it snaps to the cut, and lifted into a building's frame it becomes that building's piece. A scaffold is pieces too: poles, planks and ladders, saved and struck like any other.

Whichever way a house went up, it is made of the same pieces a hand-built house is (player-building), so the game keeps one kind of building, not three. A blueprint changed later never reprints or rebuilds a house already standing: what was built is what the save holds.
