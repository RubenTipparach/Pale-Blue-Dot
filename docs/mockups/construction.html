Cranes and Printers

All Pale Blue Dot mockups (the tracker)

"Would like to see what more advanced fabricators look like for building large items, cranes and 3d printers for assembling buildings"The owner, reviewing Forge and Fabricator (M3) on the tracker, 2026-11-12

Past the fabricator, a yard for what is too big to carry. An assembly bay makes large items and hands each over as a crate standing in the world, not a stack in the pack. A hex printer prints a building into the plot's own cells, one 1 m layer a pass, out of the same pieces a town is made of. A tower crane you drive from its cab, and a gantry in the bay, lift what is made to where it goes, as far as the load chart lets them.

About the art. The game's own (byte-identical copies in construction/game/, see its README): the ground is the terrain atlas's fields sheet (assets/tilesets/atlas.png) on 2.833 m hex columns with 1 m layers, the plot topped with its crumbly dirt tile; the apron, the bay floor, the crane's footing, the laydown pad, the bay's brick and slate, the office's boards, the rails and masts are the settlement textures flag, stone, rubble, brick, slate, boards, iron, plank and timber at their repeat_m; the hand, the shovel and the lantern post in the hotbar are the game's icons. Mockup-only: the machine icons and the printstone, crane paint, lattice, crate, container, counterweight, hazard and plate tiles are pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit (tools/art/construction_art.py), 32 x 32 and sampled nearest; the recipe item icons are the crafting mockup's. The printed building is the town's cut: walls on cell edges, floors in cells, three layers a storey.

1The yard

Drag to look around, scroll or pinch to zoom. The print slider runs the building from a bare plot to its roof in eight passes, one layer each; Play runs it. The crane's controls are its cab's: slew, trolley and hoist. Pick a load and run the trolley out: the load chart stops it where the load is more than the crane may lift there. At night every floodlight, lantern, window, the print head and the crane's red lights light what is near them, wherever the camera is, and the ground between them stays dark.

2The assembly bay

A large fabricator in a shed with its own bridge crane. Its recipes are big: each makes one item with a mass and a footprint in cells, delivered as a crate on the bay floor for a crane, a cart or a vehicle to take. It draws power from the grid (Pits, Plants and Power) and its inputs are the fabricator's (Forge and Fabricator). Queue a few; finished ones can be hooked on the tower crane above. Time runs a minute a second.

The bay's queue

One item at a time, in order. A crate stands where it was made until something moves it; it is saved where it stands.

3The printer's pass plan

One pass is one 1 m layer, seen from above. The head traces every wall line of the layer, eight beads of 12.5 cm on each, and travels between lines without printing (dashed). Walls are 0.3 m thick on the cell edges the blueprint names, with gaps where a door or a window goes; floors fill the cells. The stairwell cell is left open for a stair the crane sets in.

A printed layer is a world edit

Each finished layer enters the durable path as one edit, with the printer named as its author on behalf of its owner. A town's growth, the player's hand and the printer take the same path, and a change the world makes never overwrites the player's.

A stopped print keeps its layers

Out of printstone, out of power, or the game closed: the layers that finished are real and saved. The one under way is not, and the head lays it again from its start when the print resumes.

A crate is a record

A large item has a stable ID, a mass and a footprint in cells, and is saved where it stands. Set down, it snaps to the cut. Lifted into a printed building's frame it becomes that building's piece, a wall panel on its edge, a stair in its well.

A printed building is made of the same pieces a hand-built house is (player-building), so the game keeps one kind of building, not two. A blueprint changed later never reprints a building already standing: what was printed is what the save holds.
