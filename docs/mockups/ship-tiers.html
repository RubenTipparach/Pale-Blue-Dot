Rockets to Starships

All Pale Blue Dot mockups (the tracker)

Three tiers of ship, each unlocked by the next rung of the power ladder. Chemical and nuclear rockets are stacked from hex cells in the assembly building and fly to orbit on thrust-to-weight and delta-v. Fusion ships stay in space, fly courses their nav computer plots, run freight on their own, and meet landers in low orbit. Antimatter makes the lander the ship that goes anywhere, and a warp drive opens the neighbouring stars.

One thing here needs your call, and it is the reason for the screenshots in the survey. CLAUDE.md says ships fly Newtonian with local gravity falloff and that patched conics, maneuver nodes and transfer windows stay out. The chemical tier's "orbital stuff" pulls against that. Tab 2 flies the same rocket both ways: today's bounded field and the inverse-square field Tenebris flies its rockets in. The recommendation is at the bottom of the page.

The ladder, from the boats to the stars

The tiers follow the power the player has built (the industry mockup's grid: coal, wind and hydro, then the fission reactor; the fusion plant and the antimatter collider are its later rungs). Each tier adds parts; nothing is taken away, so a chemical rocket still flies in the antimatter age.

For the owner

"The orbital mechanics in Tenebris rust was pretty cool"

What Tenebris does (read from tenebris-rs/crates/tenebris-core): planets and moons ride circular rails, and for physics they are parked at t = 0 (orbit.rs, nav.rs: "the bodies are parked"). The rocket is integrated Newtonian (semi-implicit Euler) in orbital_gravity_at, the sum of true inverse-square pull from every body plus the star, uncapped. On top of that it draws patched conics: Conic::from_state builds the orbit from position and velocity, Kepler's equation is solved by Newton's method, the sphere of influence is whichever body pulls hardest, and the drawn path hops up to 5 SOIs over 2 hours. It has maneuver nodes (click the path, drag prograde, normal and radial handles), an Ap/Pe/T readout, a navball, and SAS hold modes (prograde, normal, radial, target, node). There is no autopilot that flies a burn.

What this game says today. CLAUDE.md, "Non-negotiable world and flight invariants": "Ships use local gravity falloff, Newtonian motion ... Do not restore rocket patched conics, maneuver nodes, transfer-window requirements, or n-body ship simulation." The flight spec's first requirement, "No orbital rocket mechanics", says the same, and the gravity-model change adopted Tenebris's bounded anchor field (full pull to 1.4 R, nothing past 1.8 R) for ships, without its orbital field.

Where the art comes from

The game's own, and what is new

The game's own (byte-identical copies in ship-tiers/game/, see its README): every hex column, on the launch field, the landing sites and each globe from orbit, wears the terrain atlas (assets/tilesets/atlas.png; fields, lunar regolith, basalt, frozen, desert, jungle and mountain sheets) on 2.833 m cells with 1 m layers; the pad, gantry, apron, rim and hall are the settlement textures iron, granite, stone and slate at their repeat_m; the Kestrel by the pad is the game's model; hotbar items are the game's icons. Mockup-only: the part icons and part textures are pixel art in the solitaire collection's style, drawn by tools/art/ship_tiers_art.py with the balatro-pixel-art kit (32 x 32, Sweaft-64, sampled nearest with no mipmaps). Rockets, landers, freighters and the starship are hex cells built by the page from those parts. Globes from orbit use cells 64 times wider than the game's so they read at that distance; the orbit, the moon's distance and every number in tab 2 are to scale. Water and the air shell are flat tints, as the game draws them.
