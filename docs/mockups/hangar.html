Hangar and Chibi Aircraft

All Pale Blue Dot mockups (the tracker)

About this mockup: the owner's words, the idea, and which art is the game's

The owner: "more mockups for planes, helicopters, vtols. make air vehicles these cute little chibi versions. they require a hangar, and the player has a ui similar to your ship builder that lets them put these together". So: a hangar on the edge of a field, five little aircraft, and a builder. Every number an aircraft flies with follows from its parts and the game's own gravity of 25 m/s², and each one is flown by the same foil model the game's Kestrel already flies by.

About the art. The game's own (byte-identical copies in hangar/game/, see its README): the hex ground is the terrain atlas's fields sheet (assets/tilesets/atlas.png) on 2.833 m hex columns with 1 m layers; the apron, the helipad, the hangar's walls and roof, the struts and masts are the settlement textures granite, flag, stone, slate, iron and plank at their repeat_m; the Kestrel on the apron is the game's model (assets/models/vehicles/kestrel); the hand in the hotbar is the game's icon. Mockup-only: the part icons, the livery paints, the shark mouth, the three roundels, the digits, the canopy glass, rotor blades, tyres, the roller doors, the runway's asphalt and markings and the windsock are pixel art drawn in code with the balatro-pixel-art kit, the solitaire collection's style (tools/art/hangar_art.py): 32 x 32 sprites shown at whole-number scales, 32 x 32 tiles sampled nearest with no mipmaps. The aircraft are octagonal prisms and boxes built from the builder's own grid, so what you draw below is exactly what rolls out of the door. The roundels are the player's own; there is no real air force's insignia here.

1The airfield

What this shows, how to fly, and the keys

A hangar of three bays with its roller doors up, an apron, a 230 m runway, a helipad, a windsock and a little tower with its beacon. Sharky and Gull are in their bays; Puffin and Dart wait on the apron; Bumble sits on the pad; the game's own Kestrel is parked beside them for scale. Drag to look around, scroll or pinch to zoom. Fly it takes the craft you are looking at (or your design from the Plane builder tab) off the runway. The assists are on (decision 2a, "make this game easy to fly"): a plane starts at 70% throttle, rotates by itself at flying speed and climbs as the throttle sets; Shift and Ctrl (or R and F) the throttle, W descends and S climbs, A and D bank it into a turn up to 45° (never past 60°), and with the stick centred it flies level, trimmed, clear of the stall. Expert turns the assists off: then W and S are the elevator, A and D the ailerons, Q and E the rudder. A helicopter flies on collective (Shift and Ctrl), cyclic (W, A, S, D) and its tail rotor (Q and E). The country runs on for kilometres: hills, a coast with the sea beyond it to the north, the town of Wendle a little over a minute's flight to the east and the fishing town of Saltby on the coast to the west-north-west, both lit at night. Everything is solid: wheels on the ground are a landing, anything else hitting the ground, a building, a mast or a parked craft fast is a crash, and two seconds later you are back on the runway. Drag to look round it as it flies. At night every runway light, pad light, floodlight, hangar lamp and nav light lights what is near it, wherever the camera is, and the moon stays dim enough that they read.

2The aircraft builder: planes, helicopters, VTOLs

What this is, and how to use it

Opened at the workbench in a hangar bay. Pick a part, paint cells; right-click or the eraser clears one. Mirror painting puts every part on both sides at once. The nose faces right and port is up. A cell is 1 m: aircraft are small, so their shapes need the finer grid that a ship's 2 m modules do not. The preview is built from the grid as you draw, in your livery, and every number on the right is worked out from the parts. Each piece on the grid is drawn in its colour: the livery's base, or its trim on the cowl, fins, tailplanes, floats and wing tips. The paint brush (B) colours single pieces from the same swatches, over the livery there; right-click with it, or its livery swatch, gives a piece back to the livery. Paint is free, like the livery, so the bill of materials does not change.

2The airship builder

What this is, and how to use it

Opened at the workbench in the airship shed. An airship is too long for the 1 m grid (a chibi one is about 24 m), so its cells are 2 m. Gas cells make the envelope: it is lofted from their footprint, as tall as it is wide, about 0.7 full on the ground, so it floats up to the height where the gas fills it. The gondola and the ballast hang under it; engine pods go out to the sides; a fin on the centre line is the upper and lower fins with the rudder, a fin out to the side is a flat fin with the elevator; the mooring cone goes on the nose. Lift is buoyancy, (ρair − ρgas) V g: the game's g scales the lift and the weight alike, so a cubic metre of helium carries about 1.05 kg here as on Earth. The paint works as on the planes.

3How the numbers follow from the parts

All at the game's 1 g of 25 m/s² (SURFACE_GRAVITY_MPS2_PER_G) and its air of 1.225 kg/m³ (pbd_core::vehicle::AIR_DENSITY). These are the steady-state summary the builder can show; in flight the foil model does the work, each wing cell a foil segment and each rotor a disc of foils, as the Kestrel's are.

5The hangar's rules

Built in a hangar

The builder opens at the workbench in a hangar bay, and nowhere else. A hangar is a building the player puts up from the town's pieces (the Hex House Builder, M6) with a bay door at least 12 m wide and 6.5 m high.

Kept in a bay

Weather acts on every craft, flown or not. In a bay an aircraft is out of the wind; left on the apron it rocks on its wheels and a gale can tip it, unless it is chocked or tied down.

Out through the door

Rolled out, it taxis on its own wheels or is pushed; a helicopter on skids is towed on a dolly. The door's width caps the span, rotors included, so a big design is a reason to build a bigger hangar.

Runway or pad

A plane whose stall is under 32 m/s takes off from the runway; one with a hover margin over 1.1 lifts off any flat cell, and a floatplane from water. A runway is a strip of flat cells, so a player can build one.

6Saves, and what stays true

An aircraft is a stored record

Its stable ID, its parts grid with each piece's paint, its livery, its pose in a named frame, its fuel and its damage are saved the moment it rolls out and every time they change. Leaving the seat changes who is in it, never whether it exists.

The parts are versioned

A saved aircraft names its part set's version. A part whose numbers are retuned keeps the old ones for aircraft already built, or migrates them forward on open; a removed part stays readable. A player's aircraft never changes shape on its own.

Rebuilding refunds

Taking a part off at the workbench gives its materials back to the pack by the give order, with drops for what does not fit. A livery is free to change.
