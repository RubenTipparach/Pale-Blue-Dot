What a save holds

the world on its own is just seeded; towns and special landmarks can be generated as part of the “save” file, the same can be said about certain zones of species … over time towns can grow, buildings can be abandoned … Just know that other factors besides players can affect the massive save world the owner, 27 September 2026

The rule this plan uses: laws are derived, facts are stored. Anything that has to survive a change to the rules that made it, or can change on its own, is a fact. Everything else follows the rules, so a fix reaches every world.

Derived from the seed, never saved

  • Terrain, biomes, rivers and caves
  • Trees, clutter and texture variation
  • Water classes, and where each fish can live now
  • The candidates towns are chosen from

Stored records, made with the world

  • Sites, with their names
  • Towns, each building stored as its own definition with a state: standing, abandoned or ruined
  • Landmarks, once one is designed
  • Species zones with a name or a state, like a named fishing ground or a stock that can be fished down

Authored changes, in one journal

  • Your digs, blocks, pieces and doors
  • The world's own changes later: a house raised, a house abandoned
  • Each entry names its author, and the world never overwrites your work
  • Checkpointed by region, so a huge world loads only what is near you

exact-cell-keys
10 tasks
first, on your word

Every edit lands on its own cell

A saved dig names its cell by a 32-bit hash, and 202,571 of the 41.9 million finest cells share a hash with another cell. Digging one digs its twin somewhere else on the planet. You said to fix it first.

What you get

  • An exact key for every cell: face, level and position packed into 31 bits.
  • The same ground: every flower, pebble and texture variation rolls exactly as before.
  • Old saves migrated once, each edit put on the cell it was made on, with the old log kept untouched.

Decided

  • The shaders unpack the key and apply the old mix, so the look cannot change. A test holds Rust and WGSL together.
  • An ambiguous old edit is settled by the ground height, then by where you were. Only a true tie goes to both cells, as today.
  • The migrated log is a new file, so an older build never misreads it.
Your gate: a video. One cell of a colliding pair dug, its twin far away dug on the old build and untouched on the new, a reload, and the unchanged ground. It is hand-made from captures, since the video tools come next.

Lights you place, and lights that come on at dusk

The engine already bakes a light field: one byte per cell, sky light and block light, with the torch as its only lamp. This step fills out a city's worth of lights and closes the two lighting changes left half open.

What you get

  • A lantern on a post, on a wall and hanging; a brazier and a candle. Each is a block with its own icon.
  • Street and wall lanterns that light at dusk and go out at dawn.
  • The player, the held tool and the ship lit by the same field as the ground, so a ship parked in a cave is dark.
  • Some flowers that glow at night.

Decided

  • Dusk-lit lamps re-bake the field at dusk and dawn, two 6 ms bakes a day, rather than a third light channel.
  • Moving things sample the field at the eight corners of their bounds each frame.
  • Light levels: brazier 15, torch 14, post and wall lanterns 13, hanging lantern 12, candle 8.
Your gate: a video. A sealed cave, a torch-lit tunnel with the player in it, the ship in a cave, each light placed and taken back, a lantern row from dusk to midnight to dawn, a candle-lit room, a brazier and a glowing meadow. Also owed: your in-game check of voxel-light, covering how dark caves are, the crease, and the falloff at a cave mouth.

climate-balance
11 tasks
placement: survey K3

The sun holds the planet at 15 °C

Today the planet freezes. It starts near +13 °C, the sea is below freezing after about 24 hours of play, and it settles near −23.5 °C, with no water warm enough for any fish in the second year. You asked for the sun to hold the average at 15 °C.

What you get

  • A planet whose average surface temperature, land and sea, holds at 15 °C.
  • Warm tropics, cold poles, seasons and weather, all still the simulation's own.
  • Fish in every year, and a climate map of a living planet.

Decided

  • First the two broken terms: the sun becomes the 1,360 W/m² the heat-loss law assumes, and clouds are retuned from about −190 W/m² of net cooling to Earth's range.
  • Then a slow thermostat on the sun holds 15 °C over the whole surface. It settles, so the sun reads as constant (K1, K2).
  • A frozen save warms back over the following game days.
Your gate: a video. Temperature maps like the one already made (docs/wiki/temperature), before and after; a time-lapse of the sea over 200 game days; the average against the day; the temperature overlay from orbit; and a fish caught in the second year.

world-map

A 2D map on M, with a live layer

The game has no map today. M cycles eight weather overlays painted on the 3D globe. This step gives it a flat map of the whole planet that the later steps add their layers to.

What you get

  • M opens and closes a map of the whole planet, zooming to one cell a pixel, with a button for each overlay (M4).
  • The base map looks like the world: each place in its ground's colour, with its towns. Biomes and the rest are overlays that grey it out (M3).
  • Live: you and your heading, your ship and parked vehicles, the night side, and the clouds and rain as they move.
  • The eight weather overlays become map layers, and can still be painted on the globe from the legend.

Decided

  • You chose: flat, like the fish range maps, at every zoom (M2). The whole map is known from the start (M1).
  • A 2,048 × 1,024 base raster built off the frame and cached per world, with tiles closer in.
  • One UI shader draws the night, the clouds and the overlays from data the GPU already holds.
Your gate, and the one you asked for first. docs/mockups/world-map.html, drawn from real rasters of the planet. It shows the biome sizes side by side, the city sites, the climate and fish layers, and the live layer, lit at night. It comes with a walkthrough video, and no map code starts until you approve it. A second video shows the map built in the game.

bigger-biomes
13 tasks
with the map

Biomes about four times bigger, with less grass

The field that splits the temperate land has a 188 m feature size, and its thresholds rarely reach desert or jungle. Fields are about 59% of the land. A bigger field alone keeps the same shares, so the thresholds move too.

What you get

  • You chose 4x as wide, about 750 m (B1), with fields, desert, and jungle with swamp about a third each (B2).
  • Fields, desert, and jungle with swamp each holding between a fifth and a half of the temperate land.
  • Old worlds keep their biomes; you start new worlds to get the new ones (B3).

Decided

  • "4× bigger" could mean the width or the area. You pick from real rasters at 188, 375 and 750 m.
  • The thresholds are tuned from the measured distribution on five seeds.
  • Each generator version is a set of config values. A save with no version reads as version 4.
  • The land, sea, rivers and mountains do not move. Only dry land's biome changes.
Your gate: pick on the mockup, then a video. An orbit pass over the old and new biomes, then a flight and a walk across desert, fields and jungle. More jungle means more trees, so you run tools/perf_suite.py before it merges.

world-persistence
16 tasks
before anything stores new state

Seeded, stored, edited: the save model

A save today is a seed, a pose, and a log of digs and placed blocks, and it is replayed whole into memory on load. It records no versions, it cannot hold a generated town, and every change in it is the player's. This step gives it the model above before any later step writes to it.

What you get

  • An identity file with every version that shapes the world: seed, topology, generator and each record kind.
  • A record store for sites, towns, landmarks and zones. A newer kind survives an older build.
  • An authored journal: you, a world process, or the world's creation. The world yields to your edits.
  • World processes prepared and tested, with none built yet: they are pure, run on the world clock, and catch up exactly.
  • Region snapshots and crash-safe checkpoints, so a world with millions of edits loads only what is near you.

Decided

  • Laws are derived and facts are stored. Fish habitat is a law. A zone is stored only once it has a name or a state.
  • Towns are generated for the whole planet when the world is made, so they can change while you are away.
  • A building is stored as its own definition. Its pieces are derived, so a fix to a stair reaches every town.
  • World time passes only while you play, so towns change while you are in the game, not while it is closed.
Your gate: a video. A dig on one cell of a colliding pair, before and after the fix, with its twin far away; a quit and reload; and the save inspector on a large save. Decided: world time passes only while you play, and the key fix ships first as its own change.

Where the towns go

The seven settlements from the towns mockup, placed on the planet by rules every machine agrees on, with your own pins on top.

What you get

  • About fifty sites to start with. Fields get walled towns and villages; desert, tundra, jungle and swamp each get their own settlement; harbours go where beach meets fields.
  • Every site on flat, dry ground, away from the twelve pentagons, and spaced apart.
  • A name for each, from its people's syllables.
  • Your pins, strikes and names in assets/config/sites.ron.
  • A small town near where you start, and the capital on another land mass (C2, C3).

Decided

  • Candidates are the 163,842 level-7 cells, screened coarsely, scored, then checked in full as each is kept.
  • A harbour needs water shelving to 6-40 m within 60 m of its shore.
  • Sites are stored records, made with the world, so a retune never moves a town you've been to.
Your gate: the mockup and two videos. The rules run in the map mockup. You drag, add, strike and rename sites, then copy the result out as RON. Once built in the game, a video flies to three sites of different kinds.

Where the fish are, and what the year is like

The fish ranges were measured when fishing was planned, but only as wiki pages. This step brings them into the game, with the year's climate beside them.

What you get

  • A fish layer: pick a species and see where a school can spawn right now, plus its all-year and part-year range.
  • "Show on map" from each species in the field guide.
  • A climate layer: mean temperature, yearly rain, sea ice and climate class, with a season slider.

Decided

  • One can_spawn function serves the spawner, the map and the guide, so they cannot disagree.
  • The climate is baked from one simulated year, about 22 minutes, on the atmosphere's own 10,242-cell grid. The file is about 0.5 MB and records what it was made from.
  • Every species on the fish map for now (K5), and five climate classes (K4).
Your gate: two videos. The mockup's layers; then the game's, including a walk to a shore the map marks for a species and a cast that catches it there. The climate is baked from the balanced, 15 °C planet.

cities-in-the-world
19 tasks, plus 27 in tenebris-towns

Towns you can walk into

First the pieces tenebris-towns designed: proper stairs, thin-wall collision, kits and doors. Then a settlement at every site.

What you get

  • Each settlement laid onto the real cells around its site, on terraced ground with edges you can walk up.
  • No two towns alike: each site rotates and mirrors its layout, picks each house's kit and leaves some plots empty.
  • Street lanterns and about half the windows lit at dusk. From orbit at night, every town is a cluster of lights.
  • No pop-in: towns fade in and out as terrain detail does.
  • An abandoned house goes dark: the first piece of towns that change over time.
  • Any boat in a harbour can be used (T7), and at night city lights light the far terrain under them (C3).
  • Mountain dwellings, cave dwellers and mound houses, once the mockup has them (T6).

Decided

  • A layout is charted onto the planet through the neighbour tables. Sites avoid pentagons, so the grid is a true hex grid there.
  • Every town is stored when the world is made. Each building is its own definition, not a template reference, so a retuned template never changes a town you've seen.
  • The terraces are generated from the stored towns, not stamped as about 10,000 edits per town.
  • A site you had already dug into is left unsettled.
Your gate: a video, and one you record. Walks into each kind of settlement, up the stairs, a door, dusk over the walled town, a fly-in from a kilometre, the night side from orbit, and a house going dark. You record the fly-in and the dusk in real time with obs-record, since pop-in only shows in real time. The gate needs every row of the towns mockup parity list built, or agreed as mockup-only.

You build them

The towns' own pieces in your hands, held to the same rules that keep a town walkable.

What you get

  • Build mode on B while on foot. The hotbar becomes the kit's pieces: walls with doors and windows, floors, stairs, posts, decks, furniture and lights.
  • Pieces snap to cell edges, cells and layer lines. A storey is three layers.
  • A ghost that turns red and names the rule it breaks.
  • A roof tool, blueprints of any house from the towns, and the mockup's overview camera with its cutaway as the build camera.

Decided

  • The towns' layout checks are split into per-rule functions that both town layouts and your placements call.
  • Building is free at first. The game has no timber, thatch or brick items yet, and the axe fells nothing that drops wood.

At every step

Written up before code

Each change is a proposal, a design, spec changes and tasks. A requirement moves into openspec/specs only with the code and the test that make it true.

Every gate is a video

You watch it before the next step starts, with the prototype playing beside the game. Each shot names what it shows. A cloud video shows looks; your real-time recording shows smoothness.

Mockups are lit at night

The map mockup's night side shows the towns' lights. The build mockup has day and night.

Frame cost is measured by you

This cloud session cannot measure frame time. Before a change that could move it merges, you run tools/perf_suite.py on the release build.

Laws derived, facts stored

The seed and the rules decide the land. Towns and named places are records. Every change after that is an authored entry, and the world yields to yours.

World time is game time

The world's clock, and everything that changes on it, runs only while you play. Towns never grow while the game is closed.

Questions go in a survey

Open questions for you live in one survey doc with options and a recommendation each, never a list in chat.

One rule, one place

The map, the spawner and the field guide share one fish rule. The towns and the player share one set of building checks.
